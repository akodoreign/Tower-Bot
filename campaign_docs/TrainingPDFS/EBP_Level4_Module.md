# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level IV — Botanical Gardens, Rookery, and Menagerie
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Connects to:** Level III (drop tubes descend from above; ropers on Level III fish tentacles down into Area 14), Level V (concealed "S" hatches in flagstone circles throughout the level lead down to the 'tween decks)  
**Primary Threats:** The froghemoth in the central lake; carnivorous alien plants throughout Area 5; an umber hulk nesting at the drop tube in Area 6; a squealer that imitates prey in distress; a living burrow disguised as a dirt mound; a gasbat colony explosive when lights are present  
**Tone:** Lush, teeming, and dangerous. A vast shipboard biosphere gone feral — the most alive place on the vessel, and therefore the most deadly. Alien birdsong echoes everywhere. Small harmless creatures scatter underfoot. The real threats hunt in silence.

---

## OVERVIEW

The moment the party sets foot on this deck, they know it is different. Noise fills the air — calls, whistles, screams, and chittering sounds from unseen creatures. The floor is covered in dead leaves, soil, vegetable matter, and bones. Small animals — birds, insects, reptiles, furred things — dart in every direction.

**Show Handout #26 or #27** (as applicable to whether the party enters from the bridge side or a drop tube) the moment they arrive.

The level was designed as a self-sustaining ecosystem for passenger recreation: a botanical garden, a rookery, a menagerie, and an aquarium. The plague ended all maintenance. The force screens that kept dangerous exhibits penned failed decades ago, and the creatures have been loose ever since. Some have gone extinct; others have bred, hunted, and established territories.

A **sonic screen** built into the ceiling prevents the sounds of this level from reaching Level III above — which is why the ropers up there never knew what was below them.

**Light Cycle.** Unlike other levels, Level IV has alternating light and dark periods rather than constant illumination. Every 12 hours the ceiling lights either switch on (bright light throughout) or off (total darkness throughout). The DM should track this. The gasbats in Area 19 are **nocturnal** and are far more dangerous during dark periods.

## GENERAL FEATURES

**Tiers.** The botanical garden occupies a large central space. Stone-faced tiers rise 5 feet each toward the outer boundary walls. Toward the center, tiers descend toward the central lake. The islet in the lake's center is tiered in 10-foot steps. All tier faces appear to be natural stone and are hidden behind thick vegetation.

**Small Animal Burrows.** Artificial burrows are built into the outer tier faces. Each is a C-shaped entry tunnel leading to an O-shaped den. A gnome or halfling can enter; larger creatures cannot without squeezing. Small latched metal doors on the serviceway side give access from behind.

**Walkways.** Flagged stone paths wind through the level. The circular flagstone resting areas have benches — and concealed hatches (marked **"S"** on the map). These hatches require a successful **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception)** check to notice, or are automatically found if the party searches a flagstone circle. They drop down into Level V ('tween decks) below.

**Serviceways.** A 10-foot-wide access passage runs between the garden area and the outer peripheral chambers. Small latched metal doors in this passage give access to the burrow dens from behind.

**Streamlets and Pools.** Thin streams wind through the level, 1–2 feet deep. The circular pools are approximately 12 feet across, tapering from 2 feet at the edge to 10 feet at the center. Life fills every pool — insects, colored fish, amphibians.

**Swamp.** Underground piping leaks badly in the southwestern section, creating a boggy morass. Water is 1–3 feet deep between hummocks of grass and reeds. Movement through the swamp costs double movement.

**Lake.** The central lake was a natural aquarium for upper-deck passengers. It still has surface fish and amphibians at its verge. Something far larger lives in its depths. (See the Cross Section of the Ship map for lake depths — the lake is 50–100 feet deep at its center.)

**Bridge.** A single span provides the only normal access to the islet (see Handouts #27 and #28). The bridge is the froghemoth's preferred ambush point.

**Islet.** A small land mass rises at the center of the lake, tiered in 10-foot steps. No path exists, but a pair of doors opens at a touch of the key plate.

**Under-Islet Marine Observatory.** A stairway inside the islet spirals down to observation floors at 50, 70, and 100 feet of depth, each ringed with thick plastiglass windows. If the ship's lights are on, viewers can see large and small fish drifting past. A successful **DC 18 Wisdom (Perception) check** reveals a fleeting glimpse of the froghemoth. Bringing **any light source** into the observatory guarantees that the froghemoth notices the party — it begins attacking the windows at 5% per round chance of breach. Attacks stop if the light is extinguished or all prey leaves its sight.

A closet on the lowest observation floor opens with a **violet card**. Inside are six sets of underwater swimming gear (1d3+1 functional; see Appendix C). A second violet card door leads to a 4×8-foot airlock. Closing the door floods the chamber and opens an outer hatch into the lake — this noise has a **75% chance** of attracting the froghemoth.

### AREA A — DEADLY PURPLE BLOSSOM PLANT

*A thick-stalked plant grows to a height of nearly 15 feet here. Its trunk is branchless and covered with scaled bark. Fern-like foliage sprouts at the top, drooping down to provide shade over a fine mossy mat at the plant's base. Mixed among the fronds are cup-shaped purple flowers with silvery stamens that point upward toward the ceiling lights. The air smells sweetly, almost refreshing.*

This is a **purple blossom plant** (see Appendix B; use Handout #31). The plant senses vibrations of any creature passing beneath its blooms, causing them to tilt and drip a syrupy poison downward. The mossy mat at the base is the plant's root system, feeding on decomposing remains.

The DM should vary the description if the party encounters more than one purple blossom plant — the fronds come in different arrangements, though the cup-shaped upward-pointing purple flowers are always the tell.

---

### AREA B — DEADLY TRI-FLOWER PLANT

*A tremendous plant with deep green stalks growing up to 8 feet in height blooms here. Each of this plant's three stalks is topped by a trumpet-shaped flower of vivid orange, bright yellow, and intense red.*

This is a **tri-flower frond** (see Appendix B; use Handout #32). Any creature within range triggers the plant's three-phase attack sequence.

**Note:** Other color variants of the tri-flower frond exist: white/pale silver-gray/pink, and golden brown/chocolate brown/russet. The DM should vary the color scheme if the party encounters multiple tri-flower fronds to keep them uncertain.

**DM Note:** Do not confuse "B" designation plant encounters with "B" (brown) color card-keyed doors. Also, "S" markings on the flagstone circles indicate concealed Level V hatches — not botanical hazards.

---

### AREA C — DEADLY SNAPPER-SAW PLANT

*A bush with broad ribbed leaves radiating outward more than 5 feet from its center grows here. Plump, delicious-smelling white berries abound at the bush's center, succulent and ripe.*

This is a **snapper-saw** (see Appendix B; use Handout #33). Hidden in the bushy center are a half-dozen tough saw-toothed stalk-leaves. Any creature stepping within the plant's reach finds these growths snap shut, holding it fast, while the saw-like stalks flail the victim to feed the plant.

The snapper-saw's berries are edible. Their color varies (transparent golden or yellow-green). Regardless of color, they are not poisonous — **4d12 berries** can be harvested from each plant.

### AREA D — THE HORRID PLANT

*A leprous yellow-gray and ugly scarlet plant grows here. Spikey leaves, bloated bottle-like stems, twitching tendrils, and writhing roots further add to its grotesque appearance. It is a horrid example of vegetable life, almost nauseating in its uncouth majesty.*

Despite appearances, the **horrid plant** (see Appendix B; use Handout #34) is peaceful and inoffensive — and potentially the party's best ally on this deck.

If any intelligent creature comes within 5 feet thinking questioning thoughts, the plant communicates telepathically, projecting reassurance that it is friendly and warning of the dangerous plants in this section (Areas A, B, C, and E). It can provide a vague mental description of the level's general layout.

**Development.** If attacked, the plant lashes its spiked tendrils. If that fails to drive off attackers, or if attacked from beyond its reach, it discharges a **Lightning Discharge** (see Appendix B). Once attacked, it will never again communicate with the party.

### AREA E — GLOBE PALM

*A tall, slender tree topped with several globe-like fruits the size of a small child's head grows here. The globes are smooth, in various shades of blue and purple. They appear very ripe and ready for plucking.*

This is a **globe palm** (see Appendix B; use Handout #35). The tree cannot directly harm — it relies on other predators. Each globe contains a pungent pheromone liquid that drives nearby predators into a hunting frenzy toward any creature marked by it.

**Development.** The globe palm drops a pheromone globe on a target passing within 5 feet. Note all nearby hostile or hungry creatures — they begin moving toward the plant's location **1 minute** after the first globe breaks, moving at full speed and attacking any pheromone-marked creature on arrival. The pheromone washes off with water or wine (1 minute of rinsing), or fades naturally after 1 hour.

### AREA 1 — LEECHOIDS

*The ground here is flooded, turning the forest floor into a large swampy morass. Patches of dry ground rise above the murky waters, creating islands of grassy hummocks among reeds, lily pads, and algae scum.*

Eight **leechoids** (see Appendix B; use Handout #36) hide beneath the murky water and beneath floating plant matter, hoping to ambush prey that rests at the water's edge.

**Development.** Any creature knocked unconscious by the leechoids' hallucinatory toxin is in danger of drowning — the unconscious creature does not hold its breath and can survive only a number of rounds equal to its **Constitution modifier (minimum 1)** before dropping to 0 hit points and beginning death saves. If rescued or if the creature breaks the unconscious condition (another creature's action or taking damage), it automatically stabilizes and no longer needs death saves.

### AREA 2 — LOW GRASS PATCH

*A 30-foot-diameter field is visible here. Lush grass grows in this meadow surrounded by tall trees and vibrant plants. It looks peaceful and inviting.*

This is a patch of **boring grass** (see Appendix B; use Handout #37) — a mutated carnivorous plant that attacks any living thing resting upon it. The blades are corkscrewed and bite into exposed flesh.

Any creature that chooses to rest, sit, or lie in the meadow is automatically attacked after **1 minute**. Creatures simply walking through the patch are safe as long as they keep moving.

**Destroying the Boring Grass.** The grass can be killed by burning oil, defoliants, a blaster, incendiary grenade, or fragmentation grenade applied to the entire patch. Lasers have too small an area of effect to damage the grass seriously. Any effective method destroys it and makes the meadow permanently safe.

**Note:** Magical protection (a *ring of protection*, *mage armor*, *armor of Agathys*) grants immunity to attacks for 1 round. The reduced movement speed from a successful attack remains until the affected creature takes a long rest or receives *lesser restoration*.

### AREA 3 — DEADLY REPTILES SECTION

*This area appears to have once been a menagerie of sorts, now neglected and reclaimed by nature. Seven sections of this space are surrounded by tall metal rods, like fence posts lacking railings. In the northernmost section, the air shimmers slightly around the rods, like heat haze. Beyond the posts in each section are shallow open caves that might have once served as animal shelters. Boulders are visible, and small creatures move in the knee-high grass.*

This was a viewing exhibit for the ship's passengers. Most force screens failed during the plague; only the northernmost cage still has its screen active (the darkened first cage). Inside it can be seen the remains of two enormous creatures — whatever was penned there died when the screens finally came back online, long after the plague.

Two **lizardoids** (see Appendix B; use Handout #38) have claimed the overgrown enclosures as their territory. They use their natural jungle camouflage to blend into the vegetation, pouncing when prey enters their space.

**Force Screen.** The remaining active screen around the northern enclosure is identical to the one in the Security Cells (Level I, Area 25A). Consult that description for details on bypassing or destroying it.

**Treasure.** The lizardoids' nest contains three unhatched eggs, numerous bones, a broken laser rifle, and a pile of dead leaves concealing a human skeleton. The skeleton carries:
- A **violet card**
- An **aerosol defoliant can** with 4 sprays remaining (each spray inflicts **2d4 necrotic damage** to a large plant or destroys a 2-foot-by-2-foot area of ground cover vegetation; see Appendix C)
- A **laser drill** with 6 charges on its power disc (see Appendix C)
- A **gold medallion** (200 gp) partially visible in a pile of lizardoid droppings

### AREA 4 — DANGEROUS ANIMALS SECTION

*This section has three enclosures marked off by lines of steel posts resembling fence posts without railings. Beyond the posts are shallow but wide caves that might once have served as animal shelters. Although once probably well-maintained, this entire section is now overgrown and neglected.*

This was a second menagerie. Its force screens have all failed. The most dangerous beast that was on display has since killed every competitor. An **aurumvorax** (see Appendix B; use Handout #39) has established this as its territory.

The golden carnivore appears to be a badger-like creature, about 1½ feet high and 3 feet long, with four legs on each side. It is deceptively small for what it is — a supremely dense predator weighing over 500 pounds. It is lurking in its den amid the overgrown vegetation, with **advantage on Stealth checks** due to the cover.

It will scuttle from hiding and attempt to Pounce on the most formidable-looking creature.

### AREA 5 — DEADLY PLANTS SECTION

*A stone pathway winds through this overgrown garden, now nearly a jungle with neglect. Curious plants with colorful blossoms, soaring trees, and vibrant underbrush sprout in dense clusters on either side of the path. The sound of small creatures scuttling through the plant life is everywhere.*

The force screens that kept these specimens penned failed long ago. Five special plant varieties are now free in this section (see Areas A, B, C, D, and E above). Multiple specimens of each type may be present at the DM's discretion.

**DM Note:** Use the horrid plant in Area D as a potential ally. It will telepathically warn the party of the dangerous plants — assuming it's been treated well.

### AREA 6 — UMBER HULK'S NEST

*One of the strange cylindrical chambers that connect to other levels is present in this darkened area. A mound of debris appears piled beside it.*

A **mutant two-headed umber hulk** (see Appendix B) has built its nest around this drop tube and claimed the area as its own. It lurks near the tube and attacks any intruder. The **two-headed variant** grants it advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks and advantage on saves against being blinded, charmed, frightened, stunned, or knocked unconscious.

**Treasure.** Hidden in the nest debris:
- A **blaster rifle** (mud-encrusted, 3 charges)
- An **anti-grav belt** (1 round of power remaining — if the wearer ascends above 30 feet or uses it a second time, the disc dies and they fall; see Appendix C, Level III)

### AREA 7 — BABOONOIDS

*There is a rustling of tree branches nearby as if some large animal has recently moved through the treetops.*

Six **baboonoids** (see Appendix B; use Handout #40) have established this area as their territory. They are currently hiding in the treetops, hoping the party passes without incident. A successful **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check** spots them.

If molested, the baboonoids hurl globe palm fruits as improvised missiles (see Baboonoid stat block) and retreat through the canopy. The band's bull has **two sleep gas grenades** (see Appendix C) which he will use if hard-pressed.

**Development.** The baboonoids have a limited vocabulary and can be communicated with on a successful **DC 16 Intelligence check** (or by any party member who has a language-translation device). They live in terror of the shambling mounds in Area 11 and desperately want them removed. If the party kills the shambling mounds, the baboonoids send **two scouts** to accompany the party for as long as they remain on the level. In exchange, the baboonoids want any grenades the party finds, as they understand their use from their experience with globe palm fruits.

### AREA 8 — SCINTILLATING PHOSPHORESCENT FISH

*A placid pool measuring 10 feet in diameter is present here. The waters are filled with dazzling, sparkling lights as if countless flashing fireflies dwelled within.*

Twenty darting 1-foot-long creatures with scales resembling semi-precious gemstones inhabit this pool. They glow both in daylight and darkness.

**Catching a fish:** Requires a successful **DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check** barehanded, or **DC 10 Wisdom (Survival) check** with a net or fishing pole. In either case, any creature handling a fish must make a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw**, taking **55 (10d10) poison damage** on a failure or half on a success. The fish's dorsal spines deliver the toxin.

**Treasure.** Each fish has **(1d10+10) × 5 scales**, each worth **5 gp** as gemlike material. A single fish yields 75–100 gp in scales on average — but getting them safely requires gloves or tools.

### AREA 9 — BROWN (BLACK) PUDDING

*A muddy hillock rises above the murky waters of the swamp, providing a brief respite from the morass.*

This hill is a **black pudding** of unusual brown coloration — indistinguishable from an earthen islet until it moves or attacks. It uses the standard black pudding stat block (see *Monster Manual*). It has no treasure.

**DM Note:** The brown coloration is a genuine surprise. Players who have fought black puddings before may not recognize this one — let them approach before it reveals itself by attacking.

### AREA 10 — BRILLIANT FISH

*A 15-foot-diameter pool lies among the vegetation at this location. Fish thrive in its shallow depths, swimming placidly about.*

*(Add if the light cycle is currently day:)* The fishes' scales flash and sparkle in the bright light, showing them to be rainbow-hued in coloration.

These fish are the same size as the fish in Area 8, but they only sparkle and flash in bright light — in darkness, they appear ordinary. Unlike their Area 8 relatives, they are voracious **piranha-like predators**. Treat as a **swarm of quippers** (see *Monster Manual*) should any creature enter the pool.

The scales of these brilliant fish are merely colorful, not gemlike — they are worth nothing.

### AREA 11 — SHAMBLING MOUNDS

*There is a rank odor of rotting vegetation here, even more pronounced than elsewhere in the swamp.*

Two **shambling mounds** (see *Monster Manual*) dwell in this part of the swamp. They lurk amongst the thick growths near the path, with **advantage on Stealth checks** among the dense vegetation. They emerge and attack when prey comes within 10 feet.

**Vulnerability Note.** These shambling mounds have **vulnerability to necrotic damage when that damage is caused by defoliants** — the aerosol defoliant spray (Area 3 treasure) and the spray gun canisters in Area 21 are highly effective against them.

**Development.** If both shambling mounds are slain, a **DC 10 Wisdom (Survival)** check of the area reveals a narrow path leading to Area 11A.

### AREA 12 — MOSSY PATCH

*A 10-foot-wide patch of moss grows in the shade alongside the pathway, free from underbrush and fallen debris. It looks soft and inviting — perfect for a rest.*

This small meadow is **green slime** (*Dungeon Master's Guide* p. 105). Anyone who pauses to rest here is in for an unpleasant surprise. The "moss" is entirely convincing until it makes contact with flesh.

### AREA 13 — SQUEALER

*The cries of an animal in pain and distress ring suddenly through the forest.*

A **squealer** (see Appendix B; use Handout #41) is lurking in a large tree nearby, emitting its imitation distress cries to lure hunters. It is spotted yellow-green with a pig-like head and enormous tusks, three sets of clawed appendages (one from each shoulder and one from its back), and a 400-pound bulk balanced in the canopy.

Its strategy: find a branch that supports its weight, squeal convincingly, drop onto the most-forward party member, and pin them with its rear limbs while biting and clawing with its forward appendages.

A successful **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check** lets a party member recognize that the distress calls sound subtly *wrong* — artificially repeated, without variation.

### AREA 13A — SQUEALER LAIR

*The wide mouth of a burrow is visible in the side of one of the tiered hills surrounding the forest. It is dark inside and the smell of death seeps from its gaping entrance.*

The squealer from Area 13 makes its den here. The burrow was originally designed for a large harmless herbivore; the squealer has expanded it. A **squealer adolescent** (see Appendix B) is inside — half-grown but fully vicious. It defends the burrow ferociously.

**Treasure.** Among the bones in the den:
- An **orange card**
- One **incendiary grenade** (see Appendix C)
- Four **100 gp gems**

### AREA 14 — LIZARD AREA

*This area abounds with lizards scampering through the underbrush, sunning themselves on rocks, and chasing the numerous insects drawn by the flowering blossoms. The reptiles range in size from finger-length to over the length of a human arm.*

Harmless lizards and lizardoids inhabit a 40-foot radius of this location in great numbers, attracted by the flowering plants and insect life. They range from chameleon-size to 3 feet and use the burrows, shrubbery, and tree branches as shelter.

**Development.** If the party has not slain all the ropers on Level III (Area 6 of that level), there is a **10% chance per minute** spent in this area that one of the ropers sends a tendril down to "fish" for lizards — or, if party members are visible from above on a higher tier, there is a **50% chance** one of them is targeted by a roper tendril. The tendril has a reach of 50 feet downward; treat as the roper's Tendril attack.

### AREA 15 — STRANGLE VINE ROOTS

*A thick knot of climbing vines sprouts from the earth here, winding up the walls to reach the level above and slithering through a nearly-open doorway into the corridors beyond.*

This is the **root system of the strangle vine** (see Appendix B of the Level III module; use Handout #21). These are the roots of the strangle vine growth that has climbed up into Area 7 on Level III. Destroying the roots kills all of that vine's central growth permanently.

**Root stats:** Use normal strangle vine statistics but with **200 hit points** and **immunity to the withdrawal effect** — the roots cannot retreat from heat. Fire and cold rules apply normally:
- Fire and laser/blaster hits deal normal damage and prevent regeneration
- Cold freezes the vines struck, but they thaw in 1d4+1 rounds unless 3 charges from a fire extinguisher are expended; 3+ charges kills a 20-foot-by-20-foot section

**Light Targeting.** The roots attack the creature carrying the brightest light source first.

**Root stats:** Use normal vampire thorn statistics but with **170 hit points** and a **10-foot reach** on its blood drain attack.

### AREA 17 — WOLF-IN-SHEEP'S-CLOTHING

*You catch movement out of the corner of your eye as one of the numerous rabbit-like creatures on this level becomes aware of your presence. Startled, it looks your way, then crouches down atop a tree stump and freezes, hoping to escape your notice.*

When this encounter takes place, roll as if on the wandering monster table, look unhappy, then read the description above and show the group **Handout #42 ("The Cute Little Bunnyoid on the Stump")**.

This is a **wolf-in-sheep's-clothing** (see Appendix B). A perfect replica of a rabbitoid sits atop what appears to be a tree stump; the "roots" are grasping tentacles; the "stump" is the creature's body; the "vines" climbing its sides are eyestalks. It uses the rabbitoid lure to attract both herbivores and the predators that hunt them.

**Development.** If any party member approaches within 15 feet of the "stump," the wolf-in-sheep's-clothing attacks with its grasping roots. At that point, show **Handout #43** (the true form) and begin combat.

### AREA 18 — THE FROGHEMOTH

*The lake lies quiet. Surface fish break the water occasionally. The bridge to the islet spans the water about 30 feet above the surface.*

The lake is home to the **froghemoth** (see Appendix B; use Handout #28 if encountered on the bridge). This is the most dangerous creature on the level — 18 feet long, 10 feet wide, yellow-orange belly shading to medium green on its back. Its nostril stalks and retracted eye appendage make it appear, from the surface, like floating vegetation.

**Bridge Encounters.** Each time the party crosses the bridge or looks over the railing, check: on a **d20 roll of 13 or higher**, the froghemoth is present and attacks. Show Handout #28.

**Lake Encounters.** Entering the water draws the froghemoth on a **d20 roll of 10 or higher**. Actively diving and exploring the depths triggers it on a **6 or higher**.

**Development.** The froghemoth does not stray far from the lake but will pursue fleeing prey for **1 minute** — only within the swampy area to the southeast. Beyond that, it returns to the lake.

**Treasure.** The froghemoth's nest is located 10 feet below the lake's surface at the point marked "X" on the map, visible from the western shore with a **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check** (must be within 20 feet). Inside:
- Bits of skulls and bones
- An **atmosphere analyzer** (30% chance functional; see Appendix C)
- A twisted, broken **laser rifle** (non-functional)
- One **poison gas grenade** (see Appendix C)
- A plastic scale model of Level IV
- A **metal chest** containing 100 small gold bars (12 gp each) — the chest has a dial that makes its contents weightless for up to 1 hour per power disc charge; capacity is 3 cubic feet

**Gems.** The lake bed is scattered with **51–100 gems** (each worth 100 gp), visible only from inside the Marine Observatory. A searcher finds **1 gem per 10 minutes** of lake-bottom searching. (An eccentric technician scattered them there from looted cargo; many are permanently lost in the silt.)

### AREA 19 — GASBAT COLONY AND ROBOBAR

*Several sinuous, comfortable-looking couches and chairs are placed about this chamber, which resembles a tavern's taproom. A large, complex collection of cylinders, tubes, spouts, and unidentifiable devices rests behind a broad bench flanked by high seats. The exposed surfaces of this expansive chamber are splattered with piles of chalky material, some of which sprout strange fungi.*

*In the air overhead drift several dozen weird creatures resembling bloated bats. They float aimlessly, but every so often, one emits a flatulent blast and briefly rockets through the air.*

One hundred **gasbats** (see sidebar; use Handout #44) inhabit this former cocktail lounge. Their chalky droppings litter the room and support colonies of strange fungi. **Show Handout #45** when the party examines the robobar (the device complex behind the counter).

**Robobar.** The bar no longer functions. A successful **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check** while examining the bar notices a small panel under a lid on the back counter with several dials and switches.

**Development — Robobar Controls.** Activating any dial or switch causes a grinding noise and a trembling that lasts 1 minute. Then a section of the outer hull slides aside, revealing a glassy surface black and flecked with colored points of light. **Show Handout #46.** The party may momentarily believe the ship has launched and they are in deep space — in truth, this is a recorded starfield display, a luxury feature that played past stellar spectacle recordings to provide "views" when the ship was in uninteresting space.

**Gasbat Light Danger.** Any light source brought into Area 19 immediately attracts **2d6 gasbats** per light source. If the light source produces **open flame**, the attracted gasbats explode on contact:
- Roll separately for each gasbat attracted
- Each exploding gasbat deals **1d6 fire damage** to all creatures within 5 feet of the light source
- **DC 14 Dexterity saving throw** for half damage
- The gasbat is destroyed

Non-flame light sources (hooded lanterns, light cantrips, torchlight behind glass) still attract gasbats, but do not cause explosions — they merely mob and obscure the light.

**Treasure.**
- **Six haste mushrooms** grow at the "X" marked on the map: rose-and-cerise branched fungi resembling coral rather than toadstools. Eating one functions as the *haste* spell for 1 minute. Baboonoids go berserk for these. Six are present (2 in 6 chance per return visit that they've been eaten by wandering wildlife).
- Behind the robobar, hidden (DC 13 Perception): a **1-gallon container of alcohol** — highly flammable, functions as a flask of oil when lit.

### AREA 20 — CREW QUARTERS

*A portion of this larger space was clearly reserved for occupation. A transparent oval table surrounded by ladle-shaped chairs stands on one side, across from a trio of cots covered with thin, shiny blankets. ### AREA 23 — CHUUL POOL

*A placid pool some 10 feet in diameter is visible here among the vegetation. A stream leaves the pool flowing to the north. A pathway skirts the pool at its southern edge. The waters appear deep and dark, and the occasional ripple indicates fish and other animal life swim in its depths.*

Three **chuuls** (see *Monster Manual*) once dwelled in the central lake, but the froghemoth drove them off. They now occupy this pool and the surrounding foliage. At least one is always present in the pool; each of the other two has a **3-in-6 chance** of being beneath the water or lurking in the foliage nearby.

The chuuls are aware of the tri-flower frond growing at the pool's north end (Area B) and carefully avoid it.

**Chuul Tactics.** Chuuls are attracted to magic. Using their **Sense Magic** trait, they identify which creature bears the most magical items or is under an active spell effect, and attack that creature before all others.

**Treasure.** At the bottom of the 10-foot-deep pool lies the partially-eaten corpse of a male high elf in tattered robes. A sodden backpack and a **staff of frost** lie beside it. The backpack contains:
- A ruined spellbook (destroyed, no longer usable)
- Two **potions of superior healing**
- A money pouch: 56 gp, 50 pp, and a **sapphire (50 gp)**

*The elf was a wizard from one of the initial exploratory parties sent to investigate raids from the Barrier Peaks. He perished here like his colleagues, slain by the chuuls.*

### AREA 24 — EXHIBIT RETENTION ZONE 1

*Numerous open-faced chambers are arranged in tiers along the verges of this area. Metal platforms, linked by ramps, climb up the tiers, ending 40 feet above the floor. Inside each chamber, a number of plate-shaped disks with dagger-sized spikes protruding from their centers are visible lining the ceiling. Most of the chambers are empty, but a few contain the bones of large unidentifiable beasts.*

*A blinking red light is visible beside a closed door at the western end of the room.*

This space was used to store animal and plant exhibits in stasis when not on active display. The spiked disks are stasis projectors. The blinking red light indicates the western chamber is **still active and irradiating its interior** — the chamber is in disposal mode, attempting to disintegrate biological material that triggered its sensors.

**Entering the active chamber** without protection exposes a creature to radiation. Each round inside: **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** or gain 1 level of exhaustion.

**The sealed door:** AC 20, 50 hit points, immunity to poison and psychic damage. Bringing it to 0 hit points causes a breach: all creatures inside and within 20 feet of the now-open door must make one **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** against radiation exposure. After this, the chamber shuts down permanently.

A **knock** spell opens the door safely from outside, but all creatures inside and within 20 feet are exposed for **1 round** as if the door were broken. A *passwall* spell fails — the walls are solid metal.

### AREA 25 — THE LIVING BURROW

*The dark mouth of a hole, perhaps an animal's burrow or a small open pit, is visible atop a low mound of grass-covered earth 20 feet from the edge of the pathway. Something glitters in the darkness of the hole, but whether it is metal or the gleam of an animal's eye is impossible to discern at this distance.*

This is a **living burrow** (see Appendix B). The creature has buried itself with its mouth open, extending its sucker-covered tentacles through the earth to resemble roots and grass. The glittering inside the "burrow" is its tongue, coated in reflective slime.

The living burrow waits until as many creatures are within reach as possible before striking — but will not allow prey to escape. It attacks immediately if any creature comes within 5 feet of its mouth.

**Show the players the cover art from the original S3 module** when they encounter this creature.

**Treasure.** Inside the living burrow's gullet are the dissolved remains of a male dwarf in partially-destroyed armor with a broken warhammer. A neck pouch contains:
- Three **emeralds** (250 gp each)
- A mithril token bearing the symbol of Moradin (500 gp)

*This dwarf was a member of one of the first scouting parties sent to investigate the metal cave. He perished here like his comrades.*

### AREA 26 — THE PEACE TREE

*A 30-foot-tall tree resembling a hybrid of pine and massive fern grows in the middle of a small meadow. Its frond-like branches are pale pink in color and droop down to nearly the ground, forming a living tent around the base. Small blue blossoms grow from the branches and at the crown. The shady grotto formed by the tree's dangling limbs looks cool and inviting.*

**Show Handout #58.** This is a rare species whose flowers produce a natural calming pheromone.

**Pheromone Effect.** Any creature coming within 20 feet must make a **DC 15 Wisdom saving throw** or become passive and nonviolent. An affected creature cannot attack or willingly cause harm to any living being. The effect lasts as long as the creature remains within 20 feet; it wears off **10 minutes** after the creature moves beyond that range. *Lesser restoration* ends the effect but requires an immediate new save if the target is still within range.

Creatures outside the 20-foot range can make ranged attacks into it, but targets inside the hanging branches have **three-quarters cover**.

**Development.** The peace tree provides a genuine safe haven — the party can take a **long rest** beneath it without random encounters. However, the effect prevents the party from using the rest productively for any violent planning (they will not feel the urge to fight anything for the duration).

**Degradation.** The tree's pheromones depend on its blossoms being fresh:
- Second visit: **2-in-6 chance** the flowers have faded (no effect)
- Third visit: **4-in-6 chance**
- After three visits: flowers are **automatically faded**; the tree is inert

### AREA 27 — EXHIBIT RETENTION ZONE 2

*Numerous open-faced chambers are arranged in tiers along the verges of this area — identical in structure to the chambers seen in Area 24. Most chambers are empty or contain bones. One chamber, however, holds not bones but a gigantic snail shell composed of a rainbow-colored pearlescent material.*

**Show Handout #59.** Like Area 24, this retention zone stored exhibits in stasis. One specimen remains: a **flail snail** (see Appendix B) in an active stasis field, overlooked in the chaos of the plague.

**Force Field.** The entrance to the flail snail's 10-foot cubical chamber shimmers faintly (heat haze effect) — this is the stasis force shield. A color card slot is set into the left-hand wall: inserting a **violet, yellow, or gray card** shuts down the field and ends stasis. The flail snail rouses in **1d4 rounds** and creeps out.

**Development.** The flail snail ignores the party unless attacked. It moves west into the garden area and begins feeding. Eventually the central computer identifies it as an invasive species:
- **1d4 days** after release: worker robots are dispatched
- If the worker robots fail, police robots are sent
- The party may be caught in crossfire between robots and the snail

**Laser Interaction.** Any attack from a technological device that deals radiant damage (lasers) has **disadvantage** against the flail snail. If the attack misses, it may be reflected or converted per the snail's **Antimagic Shell** trait — treat the weapon's spell level as equal to the number of damage dice it rolls (e.g., laser rifle doing 3d8 radiant = 3rd-level spell). Blasters that deal force damage are unaffected by this trait.

**Treasure.** If the flail snail is slain, its shell is worth **5,000 gp** to the right buyer if intact. It weighs 250 pounds.

### AREA 28 — FEED STORAGE

*The smell of rotting plant matter is rife in this cavernous space. Piles of broken containers lie scattered in unkempt piles, and many have spilled their contents — rotting, rancid animal feed. A pair of metallic creatures with cylindrical bodies, pointed heads, and long shining tentacles scurry about the space pushing plow-shaped tools, intent on cleaning up the mess. A large heap of rotted feed is piled in the southeastern corner.*

**Show Handout #60.** Two **worker robots** (see Appendix B) are cleaning up a feed storage area where an accident caused the stacked animal provisions to spill, and the feed has been rotting ever since. The robots have only recently been assigned to correct the accident.

**Robot Behavior.** The robots ignore the party as long as the party stays out of the way and does not interfere. A creature presenting a **violet or gray card** can issue understandable orders to redirect the robots' task (stop cleaning, deposit feed elsewhere), but the robots will not perform tasks for the party or leave this area. Attacking a worker robot or preventing it from working triggers it to summon a **police robot** — which arrives in 1 minute.

**Development.** After 5 minutes of work, one of the robots activates the **cargo lift** and descends to Level VI, then sweeps the feed piles into the open shaft to fall to Area 17 on Level VI below. Any creature near the lift edge when this occurs must make a **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** or be swept over the edge. The fall to Level VI is **40 feet** (14/4d6 bludgeoning damage).

*The Level VI otyughs who live in the Area 17 trash heap are the intended recipients of this waste — though they have never been formally introduced to the robots.*
