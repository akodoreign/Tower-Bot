# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level VI — Theater, Athletic, and Activity Deck
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Connects to:** Level V above (sloping passageway from Level IV/V area; drop tube terminus), Level VII below (Chapter 6 addition — biological manufacturing floor, accessible via cargo hatch and lifts from Area 16)  
**Primary Threats:** A stunted eye of the deep lurking in the swimming pool; four doppelgangers operating out of the baths; three malfunctioning physical training androids who "train" visitors to death; a mind flayer using the theater district as its hunting ground; six worker robots guarding a stasis cage containing a bulette  
**Tone:** Grim luxury. This was the ship's recreation and entertainment deck — swimming pool, gymnasium, theater, cocktail lounge — all of it now dark, decayed, and dangerous. The trappings of leisure surround genuine horror. The mind flayer knows the layout better than anyone.

---

## OVERVIEW

Level VI was the crew and passenger recreation level: sports, theater, dining, swimming, physical fitness. The plague ended all of it. Most of the level is in a state of slow deterioration — dark, littered with old bones, overrun by small harmless creatures, and partially functional in ways the designers never intended.

The level's most dangerous resident, the mind flayer, is intelligent, well-armed with salvaged technology, and has had decades to learn every corridor. It has set up a trap near its lair and maintains an uneasy distance from the two shedu in Area 11 (which it both hates and fears). The doppelgangers in the baths are a separate and opportunistic threat. The physical training androids are simply broken machines doing what they were built for, with no safety protocols remaining.

**Lighting.** The level's electrical lighting systems have largely shorted out. The primary corridors are dark; a few sections of ceiling still emit faint, flickering light (**dim light** only). Parties should expect to provide their own illumination throughout.

---

## GENERAL FEATURES

**Cargo Holds (Area 1).** Multiple large cargo spaces run along the periphery of the level, stacked floor to ceiling with containers of foodstuffs, stasis cages, and general materials. Contents are similar to the cargo areas on Level III. Several of these holds have specific encounters keyed.

**Ceiling Height.** 20 feet in the main corridors and public spaces (theater, gymnasium, pool). 10–12 feet in the smaller rooms (baths, locker rooms, crew areas).

**Flooring.** The public spaces have deteriorating carpet, cracked tile, or bare metal deck. Bones and debris are common. Small creatures — ratoids, lizardoids, insects — inhabit every dark corner.

**Audio Hazard.** Sounds travel unexpectedly on this level due to the open architecture. The mind flayer in Area 15 can hear combat anywhere in the theater section within 1 minute of it starting. The shriekers in Area 14 can be heard throughout the western wing of the level when triggered.

## ENCOUNTER AREAS

### AREA 1 — CARGO HOLDS

*A large space crammed with giant boxes and cylinders, each marked with indecipherable glyphs. The containers are stacked near the ceiling in places, arranged with a sense of order. Aisles pass through the stacked piles. Some containers appear damaged — heaps of moldering organic and rusting metallic debris spilled from them.*

These holds contain foodstuffs, stasis cages, and general materials identical to those in Level III Area 1. Several numbered encounters occur in specific cargo areas keyed below (Areas 13 and 16). The remaining cargo spaces contain nothing of value: moldering provisions, rusted components, and the occasional skeleton of a crewmember who died in the chaos of the plague.

### AREA 2 — SWIMMING POOL

*A large pool dominates this chamber. A diving platform looms at the north end over the water's deepest section. The water is murky — you can make out the bottom only vaguely. In the dim light, several human skeletons are visible on the pool floor, along with a glint of metal and the sparkle of gemstones.*

Pool dimensions: **4 feet deep** at the south end, **30 feet deep** at the north. The water is relatively clean but cloudy (filtration failed long ago). Locker rooms and dressing areas open off the north side (Area 5); tiered spectator seating lines the east side — the back section extends under the seating and connects to a hidden area where the shedu dwell (Area 11).

During the plague, an insane technician ordered a laboratory aquarium tank dumped into the pool. The worker robots complied. The tank held dozens of tiny alien predators, of which only one survived the transition to freshwater conditions: a **stunted eye of the deep** (see Appendix B). It has been fed by crewmembers coming to the pool for water and by whatever small creatures stray into its territory.

**Development.** The eye of the deep lurks in the pool's northern deep section. When the party approaches, it uses its **Illusory Eyes** ability to project a *major image* of a pile of bones on the pool floor — this image does not move or attack and is what the party actually sees. The real skeletons among the illusory ones reinforce the deception.

The eye waits. When it judges the party is at its most vulnerable (combat with another threat, party split, concentration broken), it uses its **Stunning Eye Beam** on the largest cluster of party members, then uses its claw attacks on whoever it can grab.

**Treasure.** Among the real skeletons on the pool floor:
- A **metal tube** containing rolled blueprints of the starship's complete outer hull, with an explanatory note in an unknown tongue (readable with *comprehend languages*; explains the ship's external configuration and external access points — potentially useful)
- **27 gems** (10 gp each)
- **341 pp**
- A **gem-encrusted necklace** (10,000 gp)

### AREA 3 — AUDITORIUM

*A large hall with tiered seats descending toward a broad stage at the north end. The seats are intact and in relatively good repair. A tattered curtain is drawn across the stage. Various small creatures scatter at your approach. There are some bones and old debris here, but less ruin than elsewhere.*

This multipurpose assembly hall is in better condition than most of the level — the seats are intact, the stage is largely clear. A service corridor runs along the south wall (behind the audience) with two doors leading to the stage and backstage areas (Areas 4 and 15).

**Mind Flayer Escape Route.** The mind flayer knows this room intimately. Its escape plan, if driven from Area 15, is to move east through Area 4, then circle around through the auditorium's south service doors, emerging behind the party to deliver a mind blast. The DM should track whether the party has explored and secured the south service corridor before engaging the mind flayer.

**No treasure.** The auditorium contains nothing of encounter or treasure value unless the party specifically investigates the stage, where they find old theatrical props, rotting costumes, and alien musical instruments — interesting but worthless.

---

### AREA 4 — THEATER

*This performance space is similar to the auditorium but designed for more intimate productions — live plays, concerts, comedy. Bones and old litter are present. The sounds of small things living in the backstage areas are audible. Many of the seats have been overturned.*

The theater connects east to the auditorium's south corridor and west toward the theater lobby (Area 9). The backstage area opens south toward Area 15 (the mind flayer's lair). The theater has been thoroughly looted of anything useful; only atmosphere remains.

The mind flayer passes through here regularly on its patrols and will use the theater's backstage passages during any engagement, making it an unpredictable combatant even in its "home territory."

### AREA 5 — DRESSING AND LOCKER ROOMS

*A room with little furnishings or decor. Many narrow doors line the walls, and several long low benches are arranged about the floor. Standing cabinets with narrow doors are placed in rows between the benches. Litter, old bones, and the sound of tiny scuttling feet.*

These are the pool's dressing rooms and lockers. The lockers hold only ragged swimsuits, towels, robes, and other ordinary items. Each locker room has a small shower area — the water no longer runs.

**Connection to Area 11.** The back section of the eastern locker room (behind a bank of lockers) has a partially concealed access passage connecting to the cargo hold area under the pool seating where the shedu dwell. The passage is narrow (Medium creatures must squeeze) and not obviously visible — **DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check** to notice the gap behind the lockers.

### AREA 6 — BATHS

*Humid, warm air greets you as you enter this area. The sound of dripping water echoes across tiled floors and walls. A humming sound rises as if hidden machines suddenly began to operate.*

These three bathing rooms automatically activate when any creature enters the outer bath area. All baths begin functioning: the sauna heats up, the steamroom fills with mist, the whirlpool baths begin agitating. Four **doppelgangers** (see *Monster Manual*) make their den in this area, having relocated here from Level I seeking easier prey.

**Doppelganger Tactics.** The doppelgangers wait in the sauna (Area 6A) or the steamroom (Area 6B), using the steam as cover. If they surprise their targets in either location, they gain the following special attack on the first round only:

**Knock-Out Attack (Surprise only, first round).** If a doppelganger surprises a creature and hits it with an attack during the first round of combat, the target must succeed on a **DC 14 Constitution saving throw** or be knocked **unconscious for 1 minute**. The target can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns.

**Development.** Doppelgangers will attempt to drown unconscious targets in the whirlpool baths (Area 6C) in the next round, then take the victim's place in the party. The remaining doppelgangers continue using their Shapechanger trait to confuse any survivors. Each doppelganger uses *detect thoughts* (Insight +6) before striking to confirm the identity and knowledge of the creature it is imitating.

**Treasure.** A **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check** or thorough search of the locker area adjacent to the baths discovers the doppelgangers' hoard hidden under a rotting pile of clothing:
- 12 gems (50 gp each)
- A **wound healing canister** with 4 charges (see Appendix C, Level I)
- A **paralysis pistol** with 1 charge (see Appendix C, Level I)
- A **ring** (1,200 gp)
- A non-functioning suit of **powered armor** leaning against the wall (dead power disc; cannot be activated without a new disc)
- A **small pouch** clipped to the armor's back: one incendiary grenade + one needler clip (see Appendix C)

### AREA 6A — SAUNA

*This room is filled with billowing clouds of hot steam, making it impossible to see the entirety of the space. The air is humid and hot — yet somehow relaxing.*

Automatically functions when the baths are entered. When the steam clears or the party investigates by touch, the room reveals tiered wooden seats around the perimeter and a central plinth with dark rocks in a depression — a heating element. Touching the rocks inflicts **2 (1d4) fire damage**. A control pad on the plinth adjusts heat level and introduces water onto the rocks.

The doppelgangers may be hiding here, indistinguishable from other party members or staff they've previously imitated, crouching in the steam near the upper tiers.

---

### AREA 6B — STEAMROOM

*Low benches run along the walls in stepped seating. The air here is filled with warm mist, making it difficult to see the room entirely.*

Like the sauna, this room automatically operates when the baths are entered. The steam provides **half-cover** to all creatures in the room. The doppelgangers favor this room when expecting multiple targets.

---

### AREA 6C — WHIRLPOOL / MINERAL BATH

*Four small pools, each set into the floor and filled with agitated water, occupy this room. Wisps of steam rise from the water surface.*

The doppelgangers use these pools to drown unconscious victims. One bath contains old bones and waterlogged rags from a previous victim but nothing of value. The whirlpool action of the baths counts as a strong current — an unconscious creature in a whirlpool bath cannot hold its breath and must make **death saving throws** normally once submerged.

---

### AREA 7 — GYMNASIUM

*The floor space of this large chamber contains objects of interminable purpose: horizontal poles on stands at uneven heights, padded ovals resembling the backs of horses on posts, metal rings dangling from cords, soft pads distributed about the floor. Tiered seating occupies the southern end.*

This was the ship's gymnastics and athletic competition area. Gymnastic equipment, vaulting horses, parallel bars, and seating for spectators remain more-or-less intact. Equipment and mats are piled here and there. A colony of harmless ratoids now inhabits a large stack of mats along the north wall — they scatter noisily when the party enters.

**Nothing valuable** in this room. It is useful primarily as a navigational landmark — the gym is adjacent to the storage room (Area 8) and the physical training android room (Area 10).

---

### AREA 8 — STORAGE

*This room is cluttered with various boxes, bottles, bags, and other containers, all in neglected condition.*

Storage for maintenance and custodial supplies. The containers hold inert cleaning compounds, custodian equipment, and exercise equipment components. Nothing of obvious value.

**Treasure (hidden).** The litter conceals the bones of several crewmembers who died here. Among the debris, a **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check** or 5 minutes of searching discovers:
- A **personal diary** written in Alien Common — an account of the colonization expedition and the fatal plague (readable with *comprehend languages*; provides detailed backstory about the mission, the plague's origins, and the crew's final days — valuable narrative context)
- Some worthless paper scraps
- A smashed worker robot chassis (non-functional, no salvageable parts)
- An **anti-grav belt** with 6 charges on its power disc (see Appendix C, Level III)
- Four pieces of **jewelry**: 400 gp, 900 gp, 1,500 gp, and 3,000 gp respectively

**Note.** The android in Area 10 will disarm stunned party members and toss their weapons into this room as part of its programming. The party should be aware of this if they find themselves in Area 10.

### AREA 9 — THEATER LOBBY AND ROBO SNACK BAR

*Tattered and mildewed carpeting covers the floor of this room, which bears no furnishings. At the south end is a broad high counter like a tavern's bar. Behind it is a complex collection of cylinders, tubes, boxes, spouts, and spigots — some of the spigots drip liquid and semi-congealed colorful substances.*

A **trapper** (see Level I Appendix B) clings to the ceiling, directly above the most-trafficked path through this lobby. It has been here for decades, feeding on small creatures drawn to the still-functioning robo snack bar.

The **robo snack bar** still operates, dispensing food and drink automatically when creatures approach within 5 feet of the counter. Roll 1d4 when a character eats or drinks from the bar: on a **1**, the dispensed item is poisonous (DC 15 Constitution save or become poisoned for 1 hour; no damage). Otherwise it is food or drink of bizarre alien composition — nutritious if unappetizing.

**Trapper Location.** The trapper hangs directly above the path between the lobby entrance and the snack bar counter — the natural line of travel. A creature passing beneath it must make a **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check** to notice it before entering its space. The trapper has advantage on Stealth in ceiling positions.

### AREA 10 — PHYSICAL TRAINING ANDROIDS

*A raised platform surrounded by ropes strung from posts at its four corners dominates this room. Elsewhere, the floor is covered with soft padded material. A boxing ring, and it looks lived-in.*

Three malfunctioning physical training androids are present here — once safety-programmed instructors, now lethal. Their safety protocols failed decades ago and they no longer pull their punches. They do not attack unprovoked — **they invite the party to train**.

**Initial Approach.** As soon as the party enters, all three androids approach and speak in Alien Common, indicating the party should "prepare for training" and selecting opponents. Even without translation, their intent and the gentle but insistent way they guide party members to positions makes the situation unambiguous. If the party resists or tries to leave, the androids block the exits and increase their insistence — then attack.

**The Boxing/Wrestling Android** (see Appendix B) engages its chosen opponent in boxing combinations and wrestling holds. It targets the largest or most heavily armored party member.

**The Fencing Android** (see Appendix B) engages its chosen target with its **electrical epee** (malfunctioning — delivers electrical damage for the first 6 rounds, then only physical piercing). It Disengages freely and maintains distance, targeting whoever appears most mobile.

**The Karate Android** (see Appendix B) attempts to stun and then disarm its opponent. **If stunned**, the target's weapons are disarmed and tossed into the adjacent storage room (Area 8) as the android's action on its next turn. The karate android then moves to a new target.

**The Boxing/Karate Android Rivalry.** If the karate android's current opponent communicates to it (in any language the android interprets as addressing it, or in Alien Common) that boxing is a superior discipline to karate, the karate android goes **berserk** and immediately attacks the boxing android. If these two fight, they destroy each other over **1d4 + 2 rounds**, at which point both are reduced to 0 hit points and destroyed. The fencing android continues fighting the party regardless.

**Note on the language translator.** The weightlifting android in Area 12 contains a workable language translator. If that android is disabled first, the party can use it to potentially communicate with these androids before hostilities begin.

### AREA 11 — TRAPPED SHEDU

*Two large creatures resembling winged horses with oversized human heads are present here, having cleared a space among the piled containers to create a den.*

The two **shedu** (see Appendix B) are lawful good beings freed from stasis when a malfunctioning worker robot inadvertently triggered their cage decades ago. They cannot leave the ship — the ship's dimensional force fields prevent plane shifting, and they are unwilling to travel through the dangerous levels to find an exit, knowing what lurks throughout. They have been here a very long time.

This hold extends under the tiered seating of the swimming pool area. The shedu use a small door to the north for occasional forays, and otherwise remain in their den.

**Development.** The shedu know about the mind flayer ("in the large hall-with-many-seats area" — the auditorium/theater region) and greatly fear and hate it. They will **not accompany the party** to fight it but will warn about it.

If the party is **friendly and promises immediate passage out of the ship**, the shedu reveal their treasure location (below) and warn the party about the mind flayer's location and tactics. They also know the rough layout of the level.

If the party has **greedy or dishonest intentions** (the shedu use *detect thoughts* to verify — spell save DC 15), they ignore the party and do not engage. Once attacked, they will never befriend any party member. They flee if destruction seems certain, using *dimension door* and *telekinesis* to escape.

**Treasure.** High in the piled containers in a narrow gap between them: **DC 20 Wisdom (Perception) check** to notice.
- Six **power discs** (see Appendix C, Level I)
- A **red card**

### AREA 12 — WORKOUT AREA

*This is clearly a torture chamber. Machines designed to stretch the human body into unnatural positions are arranged about the room. Two of the walls are covered with reflective glass so that no grimace of pain can be missed.*

This was the exercise equipment room: weights, resistance machines, stretching apparatus. The reflective glass walls are mirrors. The room is occupied by a **weightlifting physical training android** (see Appendix B) — deranged and dangerous.

**The Android's Behavior.** The moment the party enters, it springs into action, immediately hurling plates, dumbbells, and barbell weights at the party. It shouts encouragement in Alien Common throughout the fight:
- *"WORK UP A SWEAT THERE!"*
- *"CATCH! Butterfingers!"*
- *"LET'S HAVE SOME HUSTLE!"*
- *"You'll never make the team THAT way!"*

The android has a large supply of heavy objects to throw. It begins by hurling weights from across the room (Thrown Weight attack), then closes to melee when the range advantage is exhausted.

**Treasure.** If the android is disabled and carefully taken apart (**DC 12 Intelligence check**, 10 minutes of work):
- A workable **language translator** (see Appendix C, Level I) is extracted from its chassis

### AREA 13 — TRAPPED ANDROID AND GASBATS

*Numerous oversized crates and cylindrical containers occupy this large chamber, many of which have shifted and now lie broken and battered on the floor. In the air overhead, a few dozen curious bat-like creatures drift about like dandelion fluff on a breeze. Occasionally, one rockets off, propelled by a flatulent blast.*

**Forty gasbats** (see Level IV sidebar) inhabit this room, entering and emerging through a small opening high in the east wall (a plate blasted away and never repaired). A **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check** notices the hole, which connects to the corridor between this area and Area 12.

**Light Warning.** Any open flame light source brought into this area triggers 2d6 gasbats per round to mob the light and explode. See the gasbat sidebar (Level IV Appendix B) for explosion rules. Hooded lanterns, magical light sources, and non-flame lighting attract gasbats to swarm but do not trigger explosions.

**The Trapped Android.** A **bodyguard android** (use standard android statistics; see Level I Appendix B) is pinned under a very heavy crate that shifted and fell on it many decades ago. It has been in self-induced shutdown since the accident and activates only when noise occurs nearby.

When the party comes near, the android weakly calls for help, slowly moving an empty laser pistol back and forth as its circuits warm from shutdown. It is helpless until freed.

**Development.** If the party does not attack it, it asks for help in Alien Common (gesturing toward the crate). Freeing the android requires lifting the crate (DC 20 Strength check, or three characters working together) or a *levitate* or similar spell.

If freed, the android can be enlisted as a **loyal bodyguard** for the party for as long as they remain in the ship. It cannot be healed (the ship's android repair section is not on this module's levels) — any damage it sustains is permanent. It obeys whoever freed it.

**Treasure.** The skeleton of the official the android was guarding lies nearby:
- A **gray card** on a belt
- One **power disc**
- 20 **gems** (100 gp each)

---

### AREA 14 — RUBBISH PILE

*A large heap of moldy cloth, rotting foodstuffs, and other decaying organic matter is piled here behind a tattered curtain. Several large fungi have sprouted from the rubbish pile along with smaller toadstools and mushrooms.*

This rubbish has been deliberately gathered here by the mind flayer as a feeding ground for its "guard animals." The trash pile has sprouted **five shriekers** (see *Monster Manual*). The shriekers attract other creatures: **four piercers** (see *Monster Manual*) cling to the ceiling directly above the rubbish, waiting for the noise.

**Development.** The shriekers' noise alerts the piercers above — they drop on the loudest noise source on their initiative. The shriekers also alert the mind flayer in Area 15, which can hear them clearly through the connecting passage and will prepare its spotlight trap (see Area 15) on the **first round** that shrieking is heard, if it hasn't already done so.

**Piercers.** Use the maximum hit point variant (maximum HP of their hit dice) as described in the original text.

---

### AREA 15 — THE MIND FLAYER

*Racks of clothing, some outlandish, others resembling typical dress, fill one half of this chamber. A trio of tables, each with a mirror facing it, are arranged across the south wall. A pile of clothing lies against the west wall — an improvised bed.*

This room served as both costume storage and dressing room for the theater but is now the ship's mind flayer's lair. This creature was collected on some distant world and kept in stasis. Freed recently (by the same stasis failures affecting other creatures on the ship), it has been exploring the vessel and looking for a way home.

**Is the Mind Flayer Present?** There is a **10% chance** it is not in its room when the party arrives — it is checking on the shedu in Area 11, which it hates and fears. If absent, there is a **20% chance** per minute the party remains in the room that it returns.

**The Mind Flayer's Arsenal.** The mind flayer carries:
- An **orange card**
- A **blaster pistol** with 1 charge (see Appendix C, Level I)
- One **poison gas grenade** (see Appendix C, Level I)
- Two **sleep gas grenades** (see Appendic C, Level I)
- A **portable spotlight** (see Appendix C, Level IV)

**The Spotlight Trap.** If the mind flayer has any warning (shriekers triggered in Area 14, sounds of combat nearby, or any time it is in its lair and alert), it sets up its portable spotlight midway between the north door and the entrance to Area 15, with a **trip cord** across the north door threshold.

Any party member entering through that door must succeed on a **DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check** to notice the cord before triggering it. If triggered, the spotlight activates — all creatures facing it must succeed on a **DC 10 Dexterity saving throw** or be **blinded for 1 minute** (repeatable at end of each turn).

The mind flayer uses the spotlight to negate sight-based targeting while it attacks: ranged attacks against it have **disadvantage** while the spotlight is active and it is positioned behind it.

**Combat Sequence.** The mind flayer opens with its blaster and grenades against clustered targets, using the spotlight trap and its position in cover. If not all opponents are killed by this barrage, it moves east through Area 4 (the theater), then circles around through the auditorium's south service corridor (Area 3) to attack the party from behind with a **Mind Blast**. This ambush from behind is its favorite strategy and it will execute it whenever given an opening.

The mind flayer uses standard **mind flayer statistics** from the *Monster Manual* with its full spell suite (*detect thoughts*, *levitate*, *plane shift*, *suggestion*, *dominate monster*) plus the technology items listed above.

**Treasure.** In addition to its carried weapons and cards, the mind flayer has accumulated here:
- The portable spotlight (stored here when not in trap position)
- 37 gems (10 gp each)
- Five pieces of **jewelry** (1d6 × 1,000 gp each)
- 109 pp

### AREA 16 — STASIS CAGE CARGO HOLD

*Ten large altar-like structures are present here, each rising some 10 feet above the floor atop three sturdy legs. The altars are 10 feet in diameter and bowl-shaped. Four bent pylons loom over each bowl, ending in disks with spear-like protrusions in their centers.*

**Show Handout #51.** This cargo hold stored stasis cages for transporting dangerous specimens. It is nearly empty — the six **worker robots** (see Level I Appendix B) present have been periodically ordered by the computer to unload stasis cages over the years. The robots are currently shut down, though the central computer can reactivate them at any time.

**The Bulette.** If the party has *not* interfered with the central computer on Level I (Area 28): One stasis cage still holds a **bulette** (see *Monster Manual*). It appears to be dead — **Show Handout #51**. The cage is active and the bulette is inert.

**Development — Robot Reactivation.** For each minute the party spends in this hold, there is a **1-in-10 chance** the central computer reactivates the worker robots. If this occurs:
- The robots grind to life and begin ejecting the party through the **open cargo hatch** (a massive door in the floor of the hold, currently closed, which leads to the outside of the ship)
- If the party resists or tries to re-enter after being ejected, the robots summon **1d4+4 additional worker robots** (arrive in 1 minute) and **1d4+1 police robots** (arrive in 2 minutes)
- Once all intruders are outside, the robots **eject the bulette's stasis cage** through the open hatch and close it. (**Show Handout #52.** The bulette is freed the next round — and is now outside the ship, in the Barrier Peaks.)

**Alert Siren.** When the cargo hatch opens, an **alert siren** sounds five times and stops. This can be heard from outside the ship — creatures within five hexes of the ship can determine the direction; further creatures must succeed on a Wisdom (Perception) check (DC = 5 + 1 per additional hex) to locate it.

**Prior Computer Interference.** If the party caused a fire at the computer on Level I (Area 28, result 1 on the Minor Fire table), the bulette has already been freed and is loose somewhere on this level or Level VII. The DM should place it appropriately when the party arrives.

### AREA 17 — OTYUGH DEN

*A foul-smelling area dominated by a large mound of decaying organic material — rotted feed, trash, and animal droppings. The stench here is nauseating.*

This trash heap is the receiving point for organic waste fed down from Level IV's feed storage (Area 28 on Level IV) via the worker robots who activate the cargo lift and sweep waste into the shaft. Two **otyughs** (see *Monster Manual*) live in this refuse, feeding happily on the continuous supply of rotted organic material from above.

**Development.** The otyughs are territorial but not aggressive unless threatened or if the party comes within 5 feet of their trash heap. If the party gives them space, the otyughs will watch with obvious interest but hold back. If a party member approaches to within 5 feet of the pile without first driving them off, the otyughs attack.

If the otyughs are driven back and see the party back away, they settle back down.

**Health Hazard.** Any creature searching the trash heap must succeed on a **DC 10 Constitution saving throw** or become **diseased** as if hit by an otyugh's Bite attack.

**Treasure.** Buried in the trash pile:
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check** to find a cracked-open crate containing:
  - Four sets of **carpenter's tools** made from nearly unbreakable high-density alloys and plastics (each grants advantage on Intelligence (History) and Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) checks when used to work hard materials)
  - Two **power discs** (fully charged)

### Language Translator

*Recovered by disassembling the weightlifting android in Area 12.*

A device about the size and weight of a thick book, worn around the neck or carried in a hand. When activated (DC 12 Intelligence check, 1 success required), the device listens to any language and provides a real-time translation into whatever language the user speaks natively.

**Capabilities:**
- Translates **any spoken language** in real-time — including Alien Common, any monster languages, and all standard fantasy languages
- Range: 30 feet (must be able to hear the original speech)
- Translations are approximately 95% accurate; the DM may occasionally introduce a mistranslation for dramatic effect
- Does **not** translate written text
- Works for **8 hours** per power disc charge

**Limitations.** The translator struggles with highly idiomatic speech, metaphors, and intentional deception (the speaker's intent does not come through). It also has a **1-round delay** before translating longer sentences — in fast combat, this means the party may miss real-time commands shouted by enemies.

The device uses a standard power disc (see Appendix C, Level I).

## DM NOTES: USING LEVEL VI

**The mind flayer is the centerpiece.** All roads on this level lead toward its lair and away from it. If the party doesn't know about the mind flayer yet, the shriekers in Area 14 are the signal. If they do know, the shedu in Area 11 can provide its location and general tactics. The mind flayer's escape strategy (theater → auditorium → flanking attack) rewards parties who secure the area before engaging.

**The shedu as ally.** These are the most powerful potential allies on the level. A party that treats them with respect, proves non-hostile intent, and promises to help them escape the ship gains significant intelligence about level layout, the mind flayer's location, and a guaranteed safe area for resting (their hidden hold, which nothing on the level threatens). Getting them out requires reaching the main entrance on Level I — which they cannot do on their own given the dangers between here and there.

**The doppelgangers' long game.** If even one doppelganger escapes having killed a party member, it may shadow the party for the rest of the module. It has seen the victim's face, knows their equipment, and has all their surface-level memories. This is most terrifying in the context of a larger campaign where other NPCs will eventually interact with the replacement.

**Level VII.** Directly below Level VI (accessible via cargo hatch in Area 16, lifts, and maintenance passages) is a new level added in the Chapter 6 expansion: Level VII — Biological Manufacturing and Colonization Storage Holds. This level contains the pacifier robot bays, the biological entity production facilities, vampoids, a death-drinker, the behir, gibbering mouthers, and the source of the raiders attacking the Barrier Peaks frontier. It is the adventure's thematic resolution and can serve as a final dungeon level.
