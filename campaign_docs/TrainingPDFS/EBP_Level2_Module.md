# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level II — Service Deck 5 ('Tween Decks)
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Connects to:** Level I (drop tubes, access doors), Level III (drop tubes, cargo breach)  
**Primary Threat:** The intellect devourer — a lone stalker with telepathy and body-theft  
**Tone:** Claustrophobic. Cramped machinery corridors, an alien predator that reads minds, a dead captain's body, and three go-karts.

---

## OVERVIEW

Level II is a service and maintenance deck, not a residential floor. It runs between Levels I and III in the tight space between the hull and the occupied levels above. There are no quarters here, no vegepygmy tribes, no faction territory — just machinery, a few robots, and a very patient predator.

The party should feel watched from the moment they step off the drop tubes. The intellect devourer has been stalking this level for decades. It knows the layout better than anyone, and it can read the party's thoughts before they see it.

**Show Handout #17** whenever the party enters this level (or Level V, the lower 'tween deck).

### Layout

The 'tween decks are cramped service corridors packed with generators, transformers, power conduits, and maintenance equipment. Narrow aisles wind between machinery. Ceiling height throughout is low — around 8 feet. The access doors from the Level I and III drop tubes (brown card keyed) are the only proper entry points. The **cargo breach** (Area 6) is an improvised opening blasted into the floor during the captain's final hunt.

---

## WANDERING MONSTERS

Level II has **no wandering monster table**. The intellect devourer is the level's only ambient threat. Its presence is handled under Area 1.

## ENCOUNTER AREAS

---

### AREA 1 — INTELLECT DEVOURER

*A sinister feeling overcomes you, as if something close by is watching you with malicious intent.*

**60% chance** the intellect devourer is present in the 'tween decks when the party first enters. If absent, it is currently in the Level III cargo hold at **Area 11** (see Level III module), and will return within 1 hour.

#### Backstory

The plague drove the ship's commander to madness early in the outbreak. In his delirium, he wandered into a cargo hold and accidentally released a stasis field containing an intellect devourer — alien fauna that had been in storage, presumably as a specimen. The creature consumed the commander's brain and assumed his body.

Crewmen who were still uninfected recognized something was wrong with their captain. They organized a hunt. The chase led into the 'tween decks. One hunter managed to blast the possessed body to pieces — but the intellect devourer escaped its host and killed the crewman. That blaster discharge is what tore the hole in the floor (Area 6).

Since then, the intellect devourer has been **trapped**. The ship's energy fields prevent it from going ethereal, and its high intelligence is no help with color-card door systems. It cannot open any door on the ship. It occasionally slips through the cargo breach to hunt Level III's cargo holds, but always returns here.

It has been alone in the dark for roughly 100 years.

#### Behavior

- **10 minutes after the party enters the level**, the intellect devourer detects their thoughts (unless it is currently in Level III). It will not immediately attack — it studies the party's intentions, memories, and knowledge before deciding how to proceed.
- It is drawn to **any explosion on this level**, arriving within 1 minute.
- The intellect devourer **prefers to steal a body** over fighting directly. It will attempt to separate a single party member, render them unconscious, and use Body Thief.
- If inhabiting a stolen body, it uses that character's knowledge — including knowledge of what the party knows — and will attempt to sabotage the party from within before being detected.
- It fights openly only if cornered or if body theft is impossible.

**See Appendix B for the intellect devourer stat block.**

### AREA 2 — THE CAPTAIN'S BODY

*A skeletal body dressed in rags with curious burned holes in them lies prostrate on the floor. The skeleton's arm is still thrust through a strange bracer with two long protrusions.*

These are the jumbled bones of the ship's captain — the final victim of the devourer's original possession.

**Contents on the body:**
- **Laser pistol** mounted to a forearm bracer — **4 charges remaining** on the power disc (Appendix C)
- **Gray color card** — lying loose on the deck near the body. DC 16 Wisdom (Perception) check to notice it (drab color against the drab deck floor)

The body has no other valuables. The burned holes in the rags are laser discharge wounds — evidence of the firefight that ended the captain's last hunt.

### AREA 3 — WHEELY SLEDS

*An intriguing-looking conveyance rests here atop four large black wheels partially obscured by metal sheaths. It is the size of a small cart but lacks any sort of yoke or handle.*

**Show Handout #18.** Three wheely sleds are parked at separate locations on this level (marked on the map). Each is a **6-ft.-long, 3-ft.-wide, 1-ft.-high** battery-powered maintenance vehicle. Their controls are minimal and alien, but a DC 12 Intelligence check allows a character to figure them out (with advantage if the character spent time at the Area 5 game room shooting galleries on Level I).

**Wheely Sled Stats**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Capacity | 1 ton |
| Battery Duration | 2 hours before needing recharge (Area 4 terminal) |
| Speed Range | 10–150 ft./round (lever-controlled) |
| Steering | Flush wheel with lift-and-lock hand grip |

**Controls:** Speed lever (10 increments; each = +15 ft./round), on/off switch, steering wheel.

**Unguided.** Without a hand on the steering wheel, a moving sled travels in a random direction. Roll d8: 1=N, 2=NE, 3=E, 4=SE, 5=S, 6=SW, 7=W, 8=NW.

**High-Speed Turns.** The sled can turn 45 degrees per 6 feet of travel. Passengers traveling at **more than 50 ft./round** must succeed on a **DC 8 Strength check** to stay aboard. The DC increases by **+1 per additional 10 ft./round** beyond 50. Characters not holding on are thrown from the sled on a failed check.

**Collision Damage.** A collision deals **1 bludgeoning damage per 10 ft./round** the sled was traveling at impact to everyone aboard and the obstacle struck. **Constitution save (DC 5 + 1 per 10 ft. of speed)** for half damage.

**As a Weapon.** A sled driven deliberately into a creature deals the same collision damage. The creature may make a **DC 14 Dexterity save** to leap clear for half damage.

**Recharging.** Any of the three sleds can be recharged at the power terminal in Area 4 (1 hour to full charge).

### AREA 4 — RECHARGING REPAIR ROBOT

*A bulky metal figure with a duck-like head stands motionless in this area. A cylinder connects the metal being with a boxy protrusion on one wall. Flickering lights are visible within the cylindrical connection. There is a soft hum in the air and the smell of ozone.*

**Show Handout #19.** A **repair robot** (Appendix B from Level I) is recharging its power accumulators at a **power terminal**. It turns to face the party when it detects their presence. The terminal can also recharge a wheely sled (1 hour).

**Response by Card Shown:**

| Card | Robot Response |
|------|---------------|
| Brown, violet, or gray | Ignores the party (brown), or obeys/follows commands if communication established (violet/gray) |
| Orange or red | Alerts police robots (counter-intuitive — this robot's programming does not recognize security cards as valid for 'tween deck access) |
| Any other card | Alerts police robots |
| No card shown | Alerts police robots |

**If the robot follows the party:** Its deficient power accumulators give it **only 1 hour of operation** before it shuts down (regardless of its current charge level at time of separation from the terminal).

**Alert Response.** Two police robots arrive from Level I, Area 9 within **10 minutes** and begin searching the 'tween decks. If they locate the party without an orange, red, or gray card being displayed, they attempt to apprehend and bring them to Police HQ (Level I, Area 25).

### AREA 5 — CROSSHATCHED AREAS

*A large mass of boxes and tubes fills this area, each made of metal, glass, and unidentifiable materials. Numerous small cylinders, metal plates, meshes, protrusions, and other adornments cover every surface. Narrow aisles pass between the obstructions, their ends invisible around bends. A soft humming fills the air.*

These are **active generator and transformer banks**. The machinery is mostly well-shielded, but pockets of danger exist.

**Provoking a Discharge.** Any character who deliberately strikes, pokes, prods, or attacks these areas with a metal implement must make a **DC 10 Intelligence saving throw**. On a failure, their prodding triggers a minor explosion:
- **17 (5d6) fire damage** to everyone in a **20-ft.-diameter** area centered on the impact point
- **DC 11 Dexterity save** for half damage
- All creatures in the blast area are **Stunned for 1 minute**
- Stunned creatures make a **DC 12 Constitution save** at the end of each turn; success ends the Stunned condition

**Chain Reaction.** On any explosion, there is a **5% chance** a nearby generator (within 40 ft.) also detonates in a secondary blast:
- **12 (5d4) fire damage** in a **10-ft.-diameter** area
- Same Stun rules apply

**After Any Explosion:**
- Snapping, crackling, popping, humming for 1 minute; lights flicker throughout the level
- After 1 minute, a **worker robot** arrives to repair the damage
- If the worker robot spots the party without a brown, violet, or gray card, it summons **1d3 police robots** (arrive in 10 minutes)

### AREA 6 — CARGO HOLD BREACH

*A jagged hole some 15 feet long and 5 feet wide at its middle has been blasted into the floor here. All is dark and silent beyond the breach, giving no clue as to what might await below.*

A **DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation) check** determines this hole was created from this side by a powerful energy discharge (laser fire). It drops **15 feet** down to **Level III, Area 11** — a cargo hold containing an **occupied stasis cage** (see note below).

**Climbing Down.** No rope or handholds are present. DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check to lower oneself carefully; failure = fall and take **5 (1d10) bludgeoning damage**. A knotted rope reduces the DC to 8.

> **DM Note — Area 11, Level III (Occupied Stasis Cage):**  
> The breach drops into a cargo hold where the intellect devourer has been hunting. In this hold is a large transparent stasis cage containing **four couatls**, still in stasis. The intellect devourer freed *other* stasis creatures to eat them; the couatls were deemed too dangerous. A black dial on the side of the cage ends the stasis and frees them. If freed, the couatls do not attack their liberators. If the intellect devourer appears while the couatls are free, they will attack and destroy it — though two couatls will be killed in the fight. If the intellect devourer never appears, the freed couatls will depart the ship and fly off into the outside world.

### AREA 7 — HIDDEN LOOT STASH *(Chapter 6 Supplement)*

*A successful DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check notices a small access hatch set into the bulkhead here, nearly invisible among the gloom and grit of the 'tween deck. Chipped alien characters are barely discernible on its exterior.*

If the text is translated (Alien Common), it reads: **"Component Storage #1."**

**Trap.** The hatch is booby-trapped with an **armed poison gas grenade** (Appendix C). A DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) check notices a tiny piece of string barely protruding from the edge of the closed hatch. Disarming it:
- **DC 15 Dexterity check** with thieves' tools — success disarms the wire
- Without thieves' tools: same check with **Disadvantage** using any edged tool
- Failure or opening without disarming: the grenade detonates

**If the grenade detonates:**
- **DC 15 Constitution save** for all creatures within **10 feet** of the hatch
- Failure: **42 (12d6) poison damage** and Poisoned condition
- Success: half damage, no Poison condition
- The cloud persists for **1 minute**; any creature ending their turn within the area must save again if they haven't already

*(This is an unusually powerful grenade — the technician from Area 33, Level I modified it.)*

**Treasure (inside the hatch):**
- Three pieces of jewelry worth **1d4 × 1,000 gp each**
- Two bottles of expensive alien liquor (tasty; worth **100 gp each**)
- One **red color card**
- Two additional **poison gas grenades** (Appendix C, standard stats)

### AREA 13 — RADIATION AREAS

*A green glow pulses from the contents of this room as if everything inside were under some eerie magical effect.*

Standard radiation rooms. Apply the **Radiation Sickness** rules from Level I General Features:

### INTELLECT DEVOURER
*Tiny Aberration, Chaotic Evil*

| | |
|---|---|
| **AC** | 12 |
| **HP** | 21 (6d4+6) |
| **Speed** | 40 ft. |
| **Initiative** | +2 |
| **Proficiency Bonus** | +2 |

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 6 (−2) | 14 (+2) | 13 (+1) | 12 (+1) | 11 (+0) | 10 (+0) |

**Skills** Perception +2, Stealth +4  
**Damage Immunities** psychic  
**Condition Immunities** blinded  
**Senses** blindsight 60 ft. (blind beyond this radius), passive Perception 12  
**Languages** understands Deep Speech but doesn't speak it; telepathy 60 ft.  
**CR** 2 (450 XP)

---

**Detect Sentience.** The intellect devourer can sense the presence and location of any creature within 300 feet that has an Intelligence of 3 or higher, regardless of interposing barriers, unless the creature is protected by a *mind blank* spell.

---

**Actions**

**Claws.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 7 (2d4+2) slashing damage.

**Devour Intellect.** The intellect devourer targets one creature it can see within 10 feet that has a brain. The target must make a **DC 12 Intelligence saving throw**. On a failed save, the target takes **11 (2d10) psychic damage**, and the devourer learns the target's surface thoughts (emotional state, current thoughts, surface memories). If this damage reduces the target to 0 hit points, the intellect devourer kills the target by consuming its brain.

**Body Thief.** The intellect devourer initiates a contest against one **incapacitated** humanoid within 5 feet. The target must succeed on a **DC 12 Intelligence saving throw** or the intellect devourer magically consumes the target's brain, kills the target, and then takes control of the target's body.

While inhabiting a stolen body, the intellect devourer:
- Uses the host body's Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution scores and hit points
- Retains its own Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma scores
- Retains access to all its own traits and actions
- Has full access to all of the host's memories, class features, and proficiencies
- Cannot use features that require a brain of the original creature

The possession can be detected with *detect evil and good* or similar divination, or with a DC 18 Wisdom (Insight) check against the devourer's Deception (+2). The intellect devourer can be forced out of the host body by *dispel evil and good* or if the host body drops to 0 hit points. When forced out, the devourer appears in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the body.

---

**Bonus Actions**

**Mind Probe.** The intellect devourer targets one creature within 60 feet it can sense with Detect Sentience. Until the end of the devourer's next turn, the devourer has advantage on the first Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Insight) check it makes against the target, and the target has disadvantage on the first saving throw it makes against the devourer's Devour Intellect.

---

> **Running the Intellect Devourer on Level II**
>
> This creature has been alone for a century. It is patient, cunning, and fully aware that its only exit is through the party — if it can steal the right body and learn how to open doors.
>
> **What it knows:** The ship layout on this level perfectly. That doors require colored items. That some creatures carry those items. That the party arrived from above (from the drop tube access doors).
>
> **What it wants:** A way out of the 'tween decks, and a meal.
>
> **Ideal play:** The devourer uses Detect Sentience the moment the party enters. It spends 10 minutes reading their surface thoughts. It learns who is weakest, who has cards, who is alone most often. It will wait for a separated character, use Devour Intellect to render them unconscious, then attempt Body Thief. It then rejoins the group as a party member.
>
> **Tell:** An inhabited body cannot be detected by normal means. The DM should roleplay the possessed character normally for a session — collecting cards, "helping" the party, subtly directing them toward danger. The intellect devourer's goal inside a body is to reach a door, open it, and escape the 'tween decks.
>
> **If cornered:** It fights with Claws and Devour Intellect, attempting to debilitate the highest-Intelligence target first. It will sacrifice its current host body (by letting it die) to re-emerge and escape if the party is about to subdue it.

### Wheely Sled
*Vehicle, Large Object*

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **AC** | 13 |
| **HP** | 50 |
| **Speed** | 10–150 ft./round (lever-set) |
| **Capacity** | 1 ton (passengers + cargo) |
| **Battery** | 2 hours of operation; recharges in 1 hour at a power terminal |

Operating the sled requires a **DC 12 Intelligence check** on first use (Advantage if the character practiced on the Level I shooting galleries). After the first successful check, no further checks are needed to operate at normal speed. Checks are required again for extreme maneuvers (high-speed turns, ramming, etc.) at the DM's discretion.

The sled has no weapons system. It can be used as an improvised ramming vehicle — see Area 3 for collision damage rules.

A sled reduced to 0 HP is destroyed and non-functional.

---

### Power Terminal
*Stationary device*

Fixed recharging points appear throughout the 'tween decks. Any battery-powered device (wheely sled, personal scanner, weapon power disc) can be recharged here. A **power disc** recharges to full in **10 minutes**. A wheely sled battery recharges to full in **1 hour**. A repair robot's power accumulators recharge to full in **2 hours** if in good condition (longer if deficient).
