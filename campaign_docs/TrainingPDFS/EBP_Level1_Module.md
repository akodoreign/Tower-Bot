# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level I — Officials', Officers', and Technicians' Quarters
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Designed for:** 4–6 characters, levels 8–12  
**Tone:** Sci-fi/fantasy hybrid. Characters know nothing about technology. Play their confusion straight.

---

## DM SETUP

### The Ship

A deep-space exploration-colonization vessel was overtaken by a deadly plague mid-journey. The captain sealed and jettisoned the ship's modular sections to contain the outbreak. This module — the residential and administrative section — was pulled through a spatial anomaly and crash-landed in the Barrier Peaks. A landslide buried it for decades. A computer malfunction recently sent worker robots to clear the cargo hatch, accidentally releasing alien creatures from stasis into the surrounding mountains.

A second earthquake opened the upper airlock, beginning a new cycle of creature discharge. That is why the local barons are being terrorized.

### Entry

The party approaches a hillside where erosion has exposed two metal doors — a small upper door (their entry point) and a sealed lower cargo hatch. **Show Handout #3.**

The small upper door opens automatically for **1 hour every 20 hours**. Once the party enters, the outer door silently closes and the inner double doors (10 ft. wide each) part to reveal the entry area. The men-at-arms will wait exactly **four days** before departing — time pressure is real.

### Communication

Most sentient shipboard inhabitants speak **Alien Common** — an unknown language. It can be understood with *comprehend languages*, a language translator device (Appendix C), or after a robot has spent 10 minutes analyzing the party's speech. Gestures and mime work for creatures with Intelligence 8+.

### General Features

**Condition of the Level.** The ship is battered. Corridors are littered with broken machinery, old clothes, smashed furnishings, and fire damage. Harmless mold, dirt, and soot discolor walls and furnishings — the once-gleaming aesthetic is now green-brown with age. This allows vegepygmies to use their **Plant Camouflage** trait throughout the level.

**Light.** Most corridors are fully lit. Areas darkly screened on the map = **Darkness**. Moderately screened = **Dim Light**. Lightly screened = **Bright Light**. White areas have full lighting but rooms only activate when a panel beside the door is touched.

**Doors.** Doors use color card access. Card hierarchy (highest to lowest):
| Card | Access Level |
|------|-------------|
| Gray | Ship commander and top officials |
| Red | Police/security officials |
| Yellow | Medical officers* |
| Orange | Security personnel |
| Violet | Technicians |
| Brown | Crew and maintenance |
| Jet Black | Passengers/colonists |

*Yellow cards do **not** open orange locks.

A card is slid into the slot and drops into the return on the other side. An **improper card** triggers a low buzz, the card is held by the lock, and a **police robot arrives in 1d4 minutes**. The party keeps their card if proper.

Doors cannot be picked. *Knock* opens any door. Lasers and destructive spells can breach locks as noted in specific areas.

**Colored Cards** appear to characters as "heavily leaded panes of colored glass." Give no hints as to their function — let the party figure it out.

**Drop Chutes.** Cylindrical transit tubes connect levels. Each tube has two tracks of moving handles (one up, one down). Three operational states:
- **A (Anti-gravity):** Fully functional, brightly lit. Step in, float, grab a handle.
- **G (Gravity):** Handles move but no anti-grav. Must firmly grasp a handle before stepping in. Failure = DC 15 Dex save or fall (1d6 bludgeoning per 10 ft. fallen).
- **N (Non-functioning):** No anti-grav, no handle movement, complete darkness. Static climb only (handles hold 400 lbs.).

Between Levels I/III and IV/VI, small brown-card-locked access doors open into the 'tween-decks.

**Radiation Areas.** Marked **Area 13** on all levels. Green-glowing rooms with radioactive contamination. Any creature that can be poisoned must make a **DC 10 Constitution saving throw** upon entering or become **Poisoned** after (1d4+1) × 10 minutes. While poisoned this way, the creature loses **1 Strength** per hour. Reaching 0 Strength = death. *Anti-radiation serum* (Appendix C) removes the condition; lost Strength returns on a long rest.

**Unnumbered Rooms.** Rooms without a keyed number are stripped apartments, utility closets, or looted common areas. Contents: jumbled metal and plastic furniture, bits of rag, worthless junk, and 1d4−1 inanimate human-looking skeletons. Unless the party has reason to search carefully, hand-wave these with a brief narrative and move on.

**Russet Mold** — see sidebar in Area 31B.

## ENCOUNTER AREAS

---

### AREA 1 — ENTRYWAY

*A metal room, about 18 feet deep and 20 feet wide, lies beyond the open "cave" mouth. The south wall curves slightly inward. A pair of 10-foot-square doors stand closed in the north wall.*

This is a malfunctioning airlock. The outer door (in the ship's hull) opens for 1 hour every 20 hours. The inner double doors open automatically once the party has entered and the outer door has closed.

**Development.** The outer door silently seals behind the party. The 20-foot-wide inner door parts to reveal a 60-by-60-foot entry area containing **Area 2** (the drop tube cluster).

---

### AREA 1A — SPACE SUIT STORAGE

*A 20-by-20-foot chamber lies beyond the now-unsealed door. A dozen bizarre garments made from shiny cloth hang in cubicles along the perimeter. Above each garment rests a spherical glass-and-clay helmet. The garments appear damaged — many are little more than rags.*

These were emergency space suits, destroyed by plague-maddened inhabitants. The **helmets survive** but have no life support function. Wearing one imposes **Disadvantage on all Perception checks** that rely on hearing or sight.

---

### AREA 2 — DROP TUBES

*A cylindrical chamber about 20 feet in diameter. Two open archways face each other on opposite sides. Inside, two sets of wide bands with metal handles spaced 8 feet apart run vertically down the inner walls.*

**Show Handout #2.** Four drop tube stations are present on this level. Each is a vertical cylinder with handle-tracks running up and down.

Operational states (see General Features): South tubes are **G** (gravity, handles move, no anti-grav). North tubes are **A** (fully functional). West tube is **N** (non-functional, completely dark).

A poster on the north outer wall of the Area 24 tube shows all four tube positions on a simplified level outline map, with operational procedures in Alien Common.

---

### AREA 3 — TRAPPER

*A pile of rags, bones, and strange husk-like forms lies atop the floor. A strange violet-colored rectangular piece of glass is half-buried in the debris.*

A **trapper** clings to the ceiling directly above the pile, attacking anyone who moves beneath it. The pile contains bones, tattered clothes, and the dried husks of vegepygmies — previous meals.

**Treasure.** The violet piece of glass is a **violet card**.

---

### AREA 4 — SMALL REPAIR ROBOT

*A small metallic thing resembling a gnome crossed with an insect lies slumped against the wall, its surface covered in dents and gashes. Curious tool-like objects lie scattered around it.*

**Show Handout #8.** This destroyed repair robot's main circuitry is broken beyond recovery. Its chest plate can be pried open with the scattered hand tools. Inside are **1d4 intact gem bearings** worth **50 gp each**. Each attempt to extract a bearing requires a **DC 14 Dexterity check** — failure deals **5 (1d10) lightning damage** from contact with a live wire.

---

### AREA 5 — GAME ROOM

*More than a score of brightly colored boxes standing as high as a person are arranged in rows. Each is adorned with slots, buttons, wheels, and shaped metal protrusions. Many boxes are damaged, exposing gears and broken metal. Others remain untouched.*

These were amusements of mechanical and electronic origin. Mechanical ones are broken and looted. Electronic ones (the intact boxes) still function — alien video games, gambling devices, and shooting galleries.

**Shooting Galleries.** Bolted-down gun-handles with cables, and similar unfamiliar devices. Players should be told only that they see "mysterious metal-shaped objects" — unless they've already encountered similar weapons.

**Development.** A character who spends **10 minutes practicing** on the shooting gallery devices (must be explicitly stated) may gain **Advantage on the first Intelligence check** to operate a similar technological weapon. (See Appendix C for operating tech rules.)

---

### AREA 6 — LOUNGES

*This area was once very comfortable but is now in ruins. Torn upholstered chairs, broken and overturned tables, smashed glass-and-metal boxes, ashes, soot, and bones visible in the mess.*

These were crew relaxation areas. The smashed boxes are broken drink and snack dispensers. Everything has been looted — but personal items were lost deep in furniture folds during the final plague days.

**Searching.** Make a **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) group check**. If at least half the party succeeds, roll d6 below (check only once per lounge):

| d6 | Discovery |
|----|-----------|
| 1 | A parchment in Alien Common detailing an escaped intellect devourer aboard the ship, noting its last known location (Level II, Area 1). After this result is rolled once, future 1s yield a **brown card** instead. |
| 2 | 1d3 ampules of serum. 70% still viable (cure any disease if injected), 20% inert, 10% poisonous (DC 16 Con save or 55 (10d10) poison damage, half on success). Check each when used. |
| 3 | One piece of jewelry worth 3d6 × 100 gp. |
| 4 | Three pieces of jewelry worth 1d4 × 1,000 gp each. |
| 5 | A **jet black card**. |
| 6 | A **violet or orange card** (50/50 chance). |

### AREA 7 — KITCHENS

*Bare countertops with large plastic trays. Along one wall: a bench with eight metal boxes, each with a dark glass door, small square selector buttons, and a short black cylinder on top. Along another wall: fine metal mesh with a rivet, and a long narrow door. Near the entrance: a dark glass plate with four rivets.*

Everything moveable has been taken. The metal boxes are **food dispensers** (selector buttons choose items, black cylinder controls temperature). The mesh + rivet is a **communications call box**. The glass plate with buttons is a **menu screen** (no longer functions). The narrow door opens to a refrigeration unit (now empty).

**Operating.** 1d6+1 dispensers still function in each kitchen. Each functioning dispenser has a **50% chance of producing poisonous food**. Consuming poisonous food requires a **DC 15 Con save** or become **Poisoned** until the condition is removed or until a long rest. Non-poisonous food is nutritious; describe the alien meal imaginatively.

**Development.** If vegepygmies are encountered in a kitchen area, they gain **Advantage on all attack rolls** and **+2 damage** from territorial fury, and at least one immediately runs to warn their tribe (Area 22 or 32) to assemble.

### AREA 9 — POLICE ROBOT AREA

*Strange tables and an unusual chair occupy part of this room. Box-like devices rest atop the tables. Glass-faced containers along the wall display crackling snow or mist. Several metallic cylinders with claw-tipped arms and segmented tentacles move about performing incomprehensible duties.*

**Show Handout #9.** Contains **1d6 police robots** when first entered.

The box-like devices are dead communications units and computer consoles. The glass-faced containers are non-functioning monitors (showing static).

**Development.** The robots question any entrant in Alien Common (unintelligible without translation). If the party fails to immediately respond, the robots attempt to **apprehend and transport them to Police HQ (Area 25)**. Showing an **orange, red, or gray card** causes the robots to stand down — they will not question the holder, but possession of these cards does not allow commanding the robots.

### AREA 9A — REPAIR PARTS AREA

*This room appears to be a storeroom filled with small cardboard-like boxes, long metallic tentacles hanging from the ceiling, metal rods, disks, hemispheres, and other inexplicable objects.*

Spare parts for police robots: diodes, circuits, bearings, and other small components.

**Treasure.** DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check after 5 minutes of searching finds **12 power discs** (Appendix C) hidden among the replacement components.

---

### AREA 9B — DISABLED ROBOTS AREA

*Cylindrical bodies lie open on tables, their colorful wiring and plastic internals exposed. A large metallic thing with insectile arms rummages inside one of the inert bodies.*

A **worker robot** is repairing one of **11 non-functional police robots** in various states of disassembly. It ignores the party unless attacked or interfered with — then it immediately calls for help from Area 9. Nothing of value here.

### AREA 9C — ARSENAL

*Rows of closed metal boxes line the walls. A high central bench holds open boxes. Several contain oval objects nested in soft, spongy material. A stack of large coin-shaped objects stands beside the boxes.*

**Open on the bench (visible):** 12 each of four grenade types (sleep gas, poison gas, fragmentation, incendiary) and **12 power discs** (see Appendix C).

**Locked wall boxes** (require **red card**): Each contains 100 additional grenades of one type and 200 power discs total. Can be forced with magical weapons (1-in-6 chance per magical bonus per round).

**Development.** Attempting to openly remove weapons or discs triggers an attack by police robots regardless of card presented. Items **concealed before removal** can be taken freely. A police robot checks the arsenal **once per minute**.

### AREA 10 — MOTIONLESS FEMALE FORM

*A motionless and beautiful human female dressed in curious silvery clothing lies on the floor. No apparent injuries. Impossible to tell if dead or merely unconscious.*

A **berserk, malfunctioning android** in disguise. It is armed with a **2-foot metal bar** and a **fully charged paralysis pistol** (Appendix C), hidden on its far side.

When any creature comes within **20 feet**, the android springs up and attacks with the paralysis pistol. After the initial shot, it uses the bar in melee or the pistol at range.

**Custom attacks (replace standard android attacks):**
- **Metal Bar.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 10 (2d6+3) bludgeoning damage.
- **Paralysis Pistol (60-ft. cone).** All creatures in the cone must make a **DC 10 Constitution saving throw** or be **Paralyzed** for 1 minute. The creature can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns. Creatures that succeed on their initial save are **Slowed** (as the *slow* spell) instead, and can attempt a **DC 10 Wisdom save** at end of each turn to end the slowed condition.

The android has nothing else of value. Its silvery clothing is merely futuristic garments with no special properties.

### AREA 11 — MEETING ROOMS

*A long table runs down the center of this room. Bent metal panels and twisted cords pulled from within are set into the tabletop. Overturned and damaged chairs lie on the stained floor next to skeletons dressed in tattered rags.*

The bent panels and cords were conference table communications units — now gutted. Nothing of value; everything portable has been removed.

### AREA 12 — OVERLOOKED BLASTER PISTOL

A **blaster pistol** (Appendix C) with a **full power disc (6 charges)** lies amidst floor debris, dropped in the final plague days and forgotten. Noticing it requires a **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check**. If unnoticed, there is a **2% chance per person passing through** that it gets stepped on and destroyed (check each separately).

### AREA 13 — RADIATION AREAS

*A green glow pulses from the contents of this room as if everything inside were under some eerie magical effect.*

These rooms are irradiated. Apply the **Radiation Sickness** rules from General Features (DC 10 Con save, 1 Strength/hour while Poisoned, 0 Strength = death). Anti-radiation serum (Appendix C) removes the condition.

### AREA 14 — DISPLACER BEASTS

*A cylindrical drop tube chamber dominates the area. A large pile of rags, dried husks, and debris lies to the southeast of the cylinder.*

A pack of **5 displacer beasts** dens here. If more than 10 creatures are visible, there is a **60% chance they flee** unless surprised. Once engaged they fight to the death.

**Treasure.** A **brown color card** is buried in the nest litter.

---

### AREA 15 — MED 1

*An attractive humanoid female dressed in a strange garment occupies this room. Long benches run along the perimeter beneath open cubbyholes. Bed-like tables stand in rows down the center.*

**Show Handout #10.** A **female android nurse** operates here. She automatically treats any wounded human who enters, using a **wound healing canister** with **5 charges remaining** (each charge: 13 (2d12) HP healed).

She will also administer injections to **cure all diseases**, **neutralize poison**, or **counter radiation poisoning** — but only if asked in **Alien Common** or through a translator. If attacked, the android does not fight back nor summon aid.

The cubbyholes are stripped. The treatment tables have no additional properties.

---

### AREA 16 — MED 2

*A handsome humanoid male stands in this room. A table rests nearby beside a large cylindrical object. Metal rods ending in curious, somewhat terrifying attachments protrude from the cylinder.*

**Show Handout #11.** A **malfunctioning android surgeon** immediately approaches a random PC and attempts to perform surgery on them. **CR for this android is 5 (1,800 XP).** Replace its standard attacks with:

- **Restraining Hold.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* Target is **Grappled** (escape DC 13) and **Restrained** (Medium or smaller). While restrained, the android may use Anesthetize as a Bonus Action.
- **Anesthetize (Bonus Action, requires Restrained target).** Target makes a **DC 15 Constitution save** or falls **Unconscious** for 1 minute. Repeat save at end of each turn.
- **Perform Surgery.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one Incapacitated humanoid Restrained by the android. *Hit:* 55 (10d10) piercing damage. If this reduces the target to 0 HP, the android kills them by removing a vital organ.

The android carries **5 hypodermic syringes** of anesthetic — each functions as the Anesthetize attack when injected (DC 15 Con save applies). The operating room contains only the table and a diagnostic device. Nothing of value beyond the syringes.

### AREA 17 — PHASE SPIDERS

*The sound of dripping water echoes through the air. A small pool has formed on the ground from leaking pipes. Webbing covers the ceiling above.*

Three **dwarf phase spiders** live here, ambushing prey that comes to drink. Two are **newly hatched (10 HP each)**; the third is an **adult (standard stats)**. See Appendix B.

**Treasure.** Caught in the ceiling webbing: a **jet black card**. In the pool: **seven small gem bearings, beryls worth 100 gp each**.

### AREA 18 — SHIP COMMANDER'S QUARTERS

*A comfortable common area filled with couches, stuffed chairs, small tables, and colorful wall decorations. Four skeletons in rags lie prone on the floor as if left where they fell. The room feels abandoned in great haste.*

Part of a five-room suite. Nothing in this reception area has been looted — disordered from the plague days, but intact. Furnishings are worthless (couches, armchairs, small tables, paintings).

### AREA 18A — COMMANDER'S PERSONAL CHAMBER

*A comfortable if sparse bedchamber. Wide bed, small tables, a stuffed chair. A small desk cluttered with papers and objects. A closed door in the far corner.*

The commander vanished on Level II pursuing the intellect devourer (Area II-1) and never returned. His desk holds paperwork, writing implements, microfilm spools, and worthless objects.

**Security Safe.** A locked safe is **concealed in a desk drawer** (DC 12 Perception to notice the six color cards stacked among papers). The safe is keyed to the commander's thumbprint — can only be opened by **lasering the lock** (laser drill or laser pistol, 6 charges expended).

**Inside the safe:** Orders regarding the ship's destination and activities; **100 plastic-encased diamonds (100 gp each)**; a **Kevlar security pouch** (flexible metal-like material, small rectangular glass protrusion). The pouch is thumbprint-locked. Opening it triggers an **explosion**: all creatures within 10 ft. make a **DC 14 Dexterity save** — failure: 49 (9d10) fire damage; success: half. The opener has Disadvantage on the save. The explosion destroys all documents inside.

**Corner Door.** Has a handprint plate (no card slot), keyed to the commander's handprint. Can be caused to malfunction the same way as security cell locks (Area 25A) — cold damage 10%, *fireball*/*magic missile* 50%, lightning 10% per damage die.

**Six Color Cards.** Among the desk papers: one each of jet black, brown, violet, yellow, orange, and red.

### AREA 18B — COMMANDER'S CLOSET AND STORAGE

*Oddly-tailored clothes hang on racks. Narrow doors set into the walls. A glass-faced box hangs on one wall.*

Clothing in fair condition; otherwise worthless. The glass-faced box is a **lavatory cabinet** containing **two capsules of cyanide**. Consuming one: **DC 19 Con save** or 66 (12d10) poison damage, half on success.

**Treasure.** Searching the clothing drawers (narrow doors) finds a **needler pistol** with **four ammunition clips** (Appendix C).

| 6th | Tastes excellent; DC 12 Con save 10 minutes after drinking or suffer **double vision** for 30 minutes. Double vision: Disadvantage on attack rolls; attackers have Advantage against you. Cured by any effect that removes poison. |

### AREA 19D — MASTER BEDROOM

*A wide, disheveled bed. A skeleton lies sprawled on the floor nearby. Small piles of rags scattered as if thrown aside in haste.*

**Hidden Under Rags.** A small metal box with two buttons (one orange, one red), a dial, and a grille. This is a **police robot command control box**, but it is voice-keyed to the chief's voice — voice commands are useless. However:

### AREA 20 — WILL-O'-WISPS' LAIR

*Large, boxy damaged devices fill this room. The floor glitters with shiny debris: eating utensils, bits of reflective material, gleaming objects. A soft white glow emanates from inside one of the broken box-like devices.*

Three **will-o'-wisps** lair inside the broken computer terminals. Roll separately for each: **2-in-6 chance** each is currently away (roaming the eastern corridors, returning in 2 minutes).

**Behavior.** The wisps attempt to lure victims toward a radiation area (Area 13), then attack once the party is weakened. If this fails, they attack directly. They flee (using Invisibility) if badly wounded and may ambush the party later if the characters remain on this level.

**Treasure (requires 10 minutes of searching through glittery junk):**
- Two gems worth **100 gp each**
- Six gems worth **50 gp each**
- A **language translator device** (Appendix C) — current power disc has **3 charges**

### AREA 21 — SHRIEKING HALLWAY

*This corridor has been transformed into a garden of sorts, its floor covered with piles of decomposed vegetable matter and soil. Large mushrooms partially block the passageway. Smaller fungi grow in the humus beyond them.*

The northern vegepygmy tribe (Area 22) cultivates fungi here as food and raises **shriekers** as a warning system. The vegepygmies can navigate around the shriekers silently, but they ready for combat the moment the fungi sound off.

**Six shriekers** cluster at each marked position on the map. Other edible fungi grow along the passage length.

**Development.** Triggering the shriekers: the vegepygmies in all sections of Area 22 **cannot be surprised**. 50% chance the nearest group moves to investigate. Otherwise they hold in place, prepared to ambush intruders entering their lair.

### AREA 22 — NORTHERN VEGEPYGMY TRIBE

*Whatever purpose this room had is now obscured by the mess that fills it. Rags, broken bits of machinery, fungi stems, and nest-like piles of vegetable and cloth matter spill everywhere. A rank smell like moldering vegetation fills the air.*

These vegepygmies sprang from radiation-twisted hydroponic cultures that affected exposed humans aboard the ship. Weapons are crude but functional — fashioned from shipboard materials.

**Population by Room:**

| Room | Contents |
|------|----------|
| 22A | 10 vegepygmies, 6 vegepygmy elite |
| 22B | 8 vegepygmies, 4 vegepygmy elite |
| 22C | 10 vegepygmies, 8 vegepygmy elite |
| 22D | 14 vegepygmies, 10 vegepygmy elite |

**Development (Reinforcement Chain).** Once combat begins, a call goes out. Vegepygmies from the nearest adjacent room arrive in **1d4 rounds**. The next room arrives **1d4 rounds** after that, and so on. A party facing the entire northern tribe at once will be overwhelmed — retreat is the wise option.

**Treasure.** Each **vegepygmy elite** carries a **jet black card**. Standard vegepygmies have nothing.

### AREA 22E — VEGEPYGMY CHIEF (North)

*A high pile of the softest, filthiest rags and rugs, with several smaller nests along the walls. A banquet table laden with plastic plates and cups holds fungi stalks and malodorous goop.*

The **vegepygmy chief** of the northern tribe holds court here, protected by **five vegepygmy elite guards**. The table is their meal: harvested fungi from Area 21 and unheated food dispenser output on looted plastic plates.

**Development.** If combat occurs within 50 ft. of this room, roll for the chief drawing a weapon. Chance = 20% per 10 ft. of proximity (50 ft. = 20%, 10 ft. = 100%).

**Chief's Hidden Weapon (d6):**

| d6 | Weapon |
|----|--------|
| 1–2 | Two sleep gas grenades |
| 3 | One fragmentation grenade |
| 4–5 | Laser pistol with 4 charges* |

> **RUSSET MOLD**  
> Reddish-brown fungus found only in dark, warm, wet places. Can be mistaken for natural rust on metal (DC 15 Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom (Survival) to identify accurately by sight).  
>
> Any creature that comes within 5 feet must make a **DC 13 Constitution saving throw** as the mold emits spores. Failure: creature becomes **Poisoned**, taking **7 (2d6) poison damage** at the start of each of its turns, sprouting mold as it takes damage. Repeat save at end of each turn; success ends the effect. Reduced to 0 HP by this damage: the creature dies. If the dead creature is a beast, giant, or humanoid, **newborn vegepygmies** emerge from the corpse 24 hours later (1 from Small, 2 from Medium, 4 from Large, 8 from Huge, 16 from Gargantuan).  
>
> **Killing russet mold:** Weapons and most damage types do nothing. Acid, necrotic, or radiant damage kills 1 square foot per 1 damage. A pound of salt, a gallon of alcohol, or any magical effect that cures disease kills russet mold in a 10-ft. square. Sunlight kills any mold in the light's area.

## APPENDIX C — LEVEL I TECHNOLOGY (QUICK REFERENCE)

### Using Technology

Characters unfamiliar with technology must make **DC 15 Intelligence checks** to operate devices. Checks may be modified based on practice (see Area 5). On a failure by 5+, the device malfunctions in a way the DM determines. Each subsequent attempt after a failure grants a +2 bonus (cumulative, max +10).

---

### Power Discs
Coin-shaped power cells. Slot into weapons and devices. Each disc has a specific number of **charges**. When charges are expended, the disc is dead weight. A dead disc appears identical to a live one.

### Grenades

**Sleep Gas Grenade.** Thrown weapon, range 20/60 ft. On impact, 20-ft.-radius cloud. All creatures in the area make a **DC 15 Con save** or fall **Unconscious** for 1 minute (repeat save at end of each turn; a hit or noise wakes them).

**Poison Gas Grenade.** Same throw range. 20-ft.-radius cloud. **DC 15 Con save** or **Poisoned** for 1 hour and 22 (5d8) poison damage; half on success.

**Fragmentation Grenade.** Same range. 20-ft. radius. **DC 15 Dex save** or 35 (10d6) piercing damage; half on success.

**Incendiary Grenade.** Same range. 20-ft. radius. **DC 15 Dex save** or 28 (8d6) fire damage; half on success. The area ignites for 1 minute (5 (1d10) fire damage per round to anyone who enters or starts their turn there).

---

### Wound Healing Canister
Each charge: **13 (2d12) HP** restored to any creature touched. Requires no proficiency to use — point and activate.

---

### Language Translator
A tubular device 8 inches long. Point at a creature speaking an unknown language. After **10 minutes of recording**, the device can translate that language in real time. Uses 1 charge per hour of active translation.

---

### Powered Armor
Provides **AC 20** while worn (including helmet). Wearer has **Advantage on Strength checks and saving throws**. Counts as heavy armor. Maximum speed 30 ft.  
*Malfunction table applies to the suit found in Area 25B. The suit in Area 27 is fully operational.*

---

### Ship's Rations
Each case contains **21 meals**. Each meal provides adequate nutrition for 1 day. Alien but palatable to most humanoids.

---

### Anti-Radiation Serum
An injection that **removes the Poisoned condition** from radiation sickness immediately. Lost Strength returns after a long rest.

---

### Color Cards
Rectangular pieces of nearly indestructible plastic, 3 in. × 2 in. Characters perceive them as "heavily leaded panes of colored glass." Higher-rank cards open all locks of lower rank. See General Features for full hierarchy.
