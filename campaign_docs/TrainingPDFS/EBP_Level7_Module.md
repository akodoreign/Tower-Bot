# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level VII — Biological Manufacturing and Colonization Storage Holds
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion
### *Chapter 6 Addition — Not present in the original S3*

**Connects to:** Level VI above (cargo hatch from Area 16, Level VI; maintenance lifts; drop tube terminus in Area 23), outside the ship (cargo bay door from Area 14 leads to the lower hull exterior)  
**Primary Threats:** A docked pacifier robot with a monitoring station that can stop the raids; a death-drinker apex predator in stasis (or loose, if the party tampered with the Level I computer); six vampoids in a cold machinery room; a behir claiming an automated assembly factory as its den; biological entity troops throughout; 8 androids mid-programming who wake hostile  
**Tone:** Industrial and clinical — the factory floor of a starship invasion. Rows of cloning vats, decanting tables, nutrient injectors. Whatever romanticism the upper levels retained dies here. This is where the raiders were made and where the threat can be ended.

---

## OVERVIEW

Level VII is the spaceship's biological manufacturing and colonization storage floor — the operational heart of the ship's capacity to conquer and populate a new world. Cloning vats, decanting chambers, DNA storage banks, android programming terminals, and a tool forge capable of producing any simple weapon or device fill the level's western reaches. The eastern sections are colonization cargo holds — housing modules, supplies, and the behir's improvised den.

The level also contains the most significant decision point in the adventure: **Area 1 holds the monitoring station for the active pacifier robot** currently raiding the Barrier Peaks region. If the party can learn to operate it, they can end the raids without ever fighting the robot directly.

The level's greatest threat, the death-drinker in Area 19, may already be loose if the party caused a fire on Level I (Area 28, computer terminal). If not, it lies in stasis — dangerous only if its cylinder is damaged.

## GENERAL FEATURES

**Lighting.** Full ship lighting is active in the western manufacturing section (Areas 1–11) — these areas are considered bright-light operational spaces. The eastern cargo sections (Areas 12–27) have intermittent or failed lighting; treat as **dim light** unless noted.

**Ceiling Height.** 20 feet in the manufacturing areas. 30 feet in the main cargo holds (Areas 12, 15, 25). 10 feet in the crew quarters and laboratory areas (Areas 18–22, 24, 26).

**Active Machinery.** Several areas contain powered equipment still operating. Random machinery sounds (clanking, hissing, humming) are constant in the manufacturing section — the Wisdom (Perception) DC to hear combat nearby increases by **5** throughout Areas 1–11 due to ambient noise.

**Radiation Cross-Contamination.** The radiation areas (Area 13) border several other spaces. Any creature spending more than 1 hour within 30 feet of Area 13 (even outside its rooms) must succeed on a **DC 10 Constitution saving throw** or begin accumulating radiation exposure. See Level V Appendix for radiation sickness rules.

### AREA 1 — PACIFIER ROBOT BAYS

*A pair of metal daises sit in the center of this broad chamber, each accessible by a wide ramp. One dais is bare. Atop the other rests a 10-foot-long oval metal chariot of strange make, bristling with rod-like protrusions and floating a few feet above the dais surface. Stout black cables dangle from the ceiling and connect to the metal chariot. At one end of the room is a small altar with a number of glass plates set into the wall above it — images flicker and dance on some of the plates.*

**Show Handout #61.** This room has two recharge/storage bays for a pair of pacifier robots (see Appendix B). One bay is empty — that robot is outside the ship leading the raids against the Barrier Peaks frontier. The second robot remains in "Ready" mode, docked and fully charged, but inactive. Nothing the party does will activate the inert robot; it requires authorization from the central computer.

**Power Cables.** The charging cables connected to the docked robot carry lethal current. Any creature striking them with a slashing weapon or making forceful contact: **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** or take **55 (10d10) lightning damage** (half on success).

**The Monitoring Station.** The altar and screens are a dedicated computer console with 12 video displays. Six shows live feed from the active pacifier robot's sensors (if it hasn't been destroyed; otherwise static). This station is the key to ending the adventure.

**Using the Monitoring Station.** The station is of **Extraordinary complexity** (the most difficult technology category; see Appendix C, Level I). Each failed Intelligence check attempting to operate it has a **50% chance** of dispatching a police robot to investigate (arrives 1d4 minutes later). A mishap (failed check by 5 or more) sends a worker robot to repair the console (arrives 1d6 minutes; summons 1d3 police robots if intruders are present).

Once mastered, the party can do one of the following:
- **Shut down the active robot:** It ceases function immediately wherever it is. Its android and biological entity support units become confused, waiting 48 hours for reactivation. After that, the androids return to the ship for new orders; the biological entities disperse into the mountains without leadership, becoming a low-level wilderness hazard but no longer threatening settlements.
- **Recall the active robot:** It returns to the ship by the most direct route, enters through the lower cargo bay (Level VI, Area 16), takes the lift to this level, and docks in the empty bay. It remains inactive until the central computer experiences another programming error — which may be months or centuries.

Either action awards the party **full experience points** as if they defeated the robot in combat.

### AREA 2 — BIOLOGICAL ENTITY PRODUCTION CHAMBER #1

*Three rows of large glass cylinders run down the length of this room, each large enough to contain an adult human — 12 total. All are filled with murky green liquid. Each rests atop a 2-foot-high metal pedestal capped with a metal top. Spidery articulated arms affixed to crossbars and tracks on the ceiling dangle above each container. The canisters are otherwise empty.*

**Show Handout #62.** This production chamber contains the equipment for cloning and growing biological entities — the disposable labor force and soldiers the ship was designed to produce on demand. Raw DNA from the storage bank (Area 9) is combined in the production laboratory (Area 7), transferred here for gestation (one week per batch), then transported to the decanting chamber (Area 4) for harvesting.

This chamber is currently vacant, awaiting the next batch of DNA.

**Sabotage Opportunity.** Each of the 12 canisters has **AC 14**, **25 hit points**, and **immunity to poison and psychic damage**. Since the chamber is idle, destroying the vats goes unregistered by the ship's computer. **Destroying all 12 earns 1,200 XP** and cripples the ship's ability to produce type one biological entities — the workers sent to Area 3 will be the last the ship produces until the vats are replaced (which requires resources the damaged ship no longer has).

### AREA 3 — BIOLOGICAL ENTITY PRODUCTION CHAMBER #2

*Three rows of large glass cylinders fill this room, but more than half are cracked and broken. The intact canisters are filled with murky green liquid and have fleshy growths resembling oversized fetuses floating inside. The floor is slick with a sour-smelling, viscous green fluid.*

This production chamber was damaged in the crash but was accidentally activated by the faulty central computer. The cracked vats ruptured; the intact ones failed to bring their contents to maturity. The surviving DNA mixed with nutrient fluid and grew into something else entirely.

A **black pudding** of murky green coloration lies spread across the eastern floor section, so thinly extended it resembles spilled vat fluid. A **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check** identifies it as something other than liquid. If not noticed, it surprises any creature coming within 5 feet.

The black pudding uses standard statistics (*Monster Manual*) but its green coloration is atypical. It is functionally identical to a standard specimen.

### AREA 4 — DECANTING ROOM

*A dozen fan-shaped metal tables are arranged in rows, each set at roughly a 40-degree angle with the broadest part closest to the ground. A raised hood encompasses the base of each wide bottom and oily-looking ropes run from each table into the floor. The ceiling is a maze of tracks and skeletal metal arms. On one table, a hulking corpse rots, producing a foul stench.*

**Show Handout #63.** Cloning vats from the production chambers were transported here, positioned above the tables, and opened — the clone and growth fluids would spill down, be collected by the raised hoods, and the fluid pumped back to storage tanks while the newborn entity was delivered to Area 5 for nutrient injections. Worker robots completed the awakening process.

The corpse on the table is a **failed type two biological entity** that died in the decanting process due to cloning defects. It has been left here, in advanced decay, infested with a **swarm of rot grubs** (see Level IV Appendix B). Anyone touching the body is attacked immediately.

**Treasure.** An emergency cabinet in the south wall (marked with a stylized fire symbol in red and white) contains:
- Four **wound healing canisters** (see Appendix C, Level I)
- One fully charged **fire extinguisher** (see Appendix C, Level III)

### AREA 5 — NUTRIENT ADMINISTRATION

*Two dozen slab-like tables are positioned evenly about this stark white chamber. Above each table is a gleaming metal post hanging 4 feet above the tabletop, bearing three skeletal articulated arms each tipped with a sharp barb. A dark glass plate is set into the side of the post.*

**Show Handout #64.** Upon being decanted, each biological entity received a single high-calorie injection here designed to sustain it through its anticipated short life of service. The injectors are of **Moderate complexity** (see Appendix C, Level I).

**Operating an Injector.** Once understood, the dark glass plate (a display) shows three menu options in Alien Common:
1. **"Baseline Injection"**
2. **"Amplified Metabolism Injection"**
3. **"Hostile Environment Deployment Injection"** (with sub-menu: High Temperature, Low Temperature, Caustic, Extreme Static, High Decibel)

A creature must lie on the table and activate the "Deploy Injection?" prompt. If no living creature is present, the injector produces an error.

**Effects on Non-Biological-Entities:**

**Baseline Injection.** DC 10 Constitution save. *Success:* The creature can survive up to **2 weeks without food** and only needs a cup of water per day. *Failure:* **35 (10d6) poison damage**, poisoned until long rest.

**Amplified Metabolism Injection.** DC 15 Constitution save. *Success:* Baseline benefits plus the creature is under the effects of a **haste spell for 1 hour**, then incapacitated until a short or long rest. *Failure:* **45 (10d8) poison damage**, poisoned until long rest.

**Hostile Environment Deployment Injection.** DC 20 Constitution save. *Success:* Baseline benefits plus *protection from energy* (type chosen via sub-menu) for **24 hours**; after that, the creature has **vulnerability** to that energy type for 24 more hours before returning to normal. *Failure:* **55 (10d10) poison damage**; if this reduces the creature to 0 hit points, it dies.

Injectors have an effectively endless chemical supply. Only one injection type can be active at a time — the most recent supersedes any prior one.

### AREA 6 — TOOL FORGE

*A bulky device comprising oval metal canisters affixed to an angled box the size of a cart occupies the center of this chamber. A flat glass-topped table protrudes from the box, its surface glowing with pale white light. A hood like a blacksmith's forge hangs 3 feet above the table. Several small metal squares and a glass plate are set into the box's side.*

This automated fabricator creates tools and simple devices using a quick-setting chemical compound. The forge is of **Difficult complexity** (see Appendix C, Level I).

**Operating the Forge.** Once mastered, the keypad and monitor allow selection from a catalog of more than **100 items**. The forge can produce up to **1,000 items** before its chemical compound tanks run dry. Rules:
- Items must be solid or have no more than three separate parts
- Maximum size: **4 square feet**
- Production time: **1 minute per item** (the hood descends, processes, then rises)
- Currently set to produce **battleaxes** (the default weapon for biological entities)

**Catalog Exploration.** Searching through the catalog takes time but reveals what the forge can and cannot make. It can produce tools, simple weapons, rope, containers, poles, hooks, and many mundane items. It cannot produce explosives, electronic devices, or anything with more than three components.

**The Gold Question.** Theoretically the forge can produce **gold bars** and **gemstones**. These look genuine at a glance — a DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation), Nature, or Perception check reveals they are synthetic replicas (essentially worthless to serious buyers, though they might fool someone for a short time).

### AREA 7 — DNA PRODUCTION LABORATORY

*High benches covered with curious glassware, transparent horn pieces, and small-tiled altars fill this chamber. Skeletal articulated metal arms hang from ceiling tracks. A glass-faced box at one end glows with blue light; two small round petri dishes are visible through the front. A metal duck-billed creature stands near a bench, performing an unknown task.*

**Show Handout #65.** A specialized **worker robot** (see Level I Appendix B) is mixing the next batch of type one biological entity DNA for another production run. It cannot be commanded by the party regardless of color card — if shown yellow or higher it ignores the party; otherwise it summons a police robot (1 minute). Any damage caused summons a police robot even if the party displayed an appropriate card.

**The Glowing Box.** A specialized stasis unit containing **two experimental life-forms** in separate petri dishes:

**Dish 1 — The Metal-Eater.** A lump of dark blue clay-like matter. It consumes ferrous metal at a rate of **1 cubic foot per feeding**, once per day. After each meal, it expels **1d6 × 100 gp** in pure gold as waste. After 10 meals it hibernates for 1d6 months, appearing dead, then revives hungry. **AC 5, 2 HP** — treat as an object. It ignores non-ferrous materials.

**Dish 2 — The Viral Petri Dish.** A vibrant red slime mold. **Do not open.** If opened, all breathing creatures within 20 feet must make a **DC 18 Constitution saving throw**, taking **55 (10d10) necrotic damage** on a failure (half on success). Creatures reduced to 0 hit points by this damage **die instantly** — no death saves. Casting *lesser restoration* or *purify food and drink* on the closed dish before opening it kills the virus and makes it permanently inert.

**Stasis Box Security.** The box has **AC 15**, **25 HP**, and immunity to poison and psychic damage. Destroying the box shatters both dishes simultaneously — both creatures are released at once.

### AREA 8 — BROKEN DOORS

*The walls, ceiling, and floor are black with soot and damaged by some explosive force. The shredded remains of a metal creature — its cylindrical body peeled open like exotic fruit — lies slumped by a door in the north wall. The damage has bent back part of the door and white light spills through the gap. A second door to the east stands completely open, leading to a darkened room beyond.*

A police robot suffered catastrophic failure of its internal grenade arsenal here. The explosion destroyed it, damaged the corridor, bent open the door leading to Area 9, and disabled the lock on the door to Area 27 (permanently ajar). ### AREA 9 — DNA STORAGE BANK

*This area is filled with rectangular glass containers arranged in rows down the length of the room. Each is lit from within, making the white walls gleam with sterile brightness. Inside each of the 10 containers are hundreds of tiny transparent slats containing a coin-sized drop of yellow fluid. From somewhere deeper in the room, the sound of breaking glass is heard.*

DNA from countless species — native, collected from alien worlds along the ship's route — are stored in these refrigerated units. Each unit is 8 feet tall × 6 feet deep × 3 feet wide, holding 1,000 crystalline DNA slides. The input keypad on each unit can summon a specific slide from an internal robotic arm.

**Occupants.** Four **type two biological entities** (see Appendix B) got into the bank through the broken doorway from Area 8 and are smashing refrigeration banks for the satisfying sound of shattering glass.

**Tactical Peculiarity.** The biological entities are momentarily perplexed when the party enters — each must succeed on a **DC 12 Wisdom saving throw** or spend 2 rounds utterly baffled before deciding to smash the party. During this perplexed state, attack rolls against them have **advantage** and they have **disadvantage on all saving throws**. These effects end the round after they are first attacked.

One of the four carries a **monoblade fire axe** (see Appendix C). It uses the following attack:

*Monoblade Fire Axe.* Melee Weapon Attack: +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 11 (1d12 + 5) slashing damage plus 2 (1d4) necrotic damage. The axe is adamantine for damage resistance purposes.

### AREA 10 — ANDROID BLANKS STORAGE

*Two tiers of rectangular niches resembling open coffins are present on either side of this room. Each compartment contains a beautiful human figure dressed in a plain white shift, lying motionless, eyes closed, showing no signs of life. A walkway accessed by ramps separates the tiers. A cylindrical metal creature with serpentine tentacles and a flat round head moves down the length of the chamber.*

This hold stores 200 **inert androids** (167 occupied bays, 33 empty). The androids have no programming — they are effectively living corpses until the ship's computer activates them. A **police robot** (see Level I Appendix B) is permanently assigned here with orders to deliver one warning, then attack with lethal force if not complied with or shown a gray card.

**The Replacement Pool.** The ship's computer uses these androids to replace destroyed units throughout the vessel. Every time the party destroys an android elsewhere on the ship and then withdraws, a replacement is dispatched from here. If the party has destroyed more than 167 androids total, no more replacements occur.

**Destroying the Blanks.** Slaughtering the inert androids is possible — they are helpless and have no defenses. Each inert android has **AC 10, 4 HP**. Destroying them here (while the police robot is distracted or defeated) prevents future android reinforcements. The party earns **50 XP** per destroyed blank and a collective **1,000 XP bonus** if all 167 are destroyed.

### AREA 11 — ANDROID PROGRAMMING CENTER

*A low raised dais sits in the center of this room with eight chairs arranged in a circle atop it. A hemispherical dome is set into the ceiling directly above the chairs, with thin cords dangling from its golden surface to the chairs below. Eight beautiful humans — an equal mix of males and females — sit in the chairs with cords attached to their brows. The room's perimeter is filled with boxy devices covered in blinking lights and whirling wheels behind glass.*

**Show Handout #66.** Eight **androids** (see Level I Appendix B) are mid-programming, currently incapacitated as the central computer updates their instructions. They are either being reprogrammed to assist the active pacifier robot or to replace damaged units on Level I.

**Timing.** The androids complete programming and become active **1 minute after the party enters the room**. Before that, they are incapacitated and can be attacked freely.

**Computer Terminals.** The boxy data stores are far beyond the party's skills to operate intentionally. Random fiddling: roll **1d6** per creature experimenting (repeat each round they persist):
- **4–6:** Lights change patterns, wheels spin differently. No harmful effect.
- **1–3:** Roll **1d4** for mishap:
  1. One android that has finished programming wakes early — it activates immediately and attacks.
  2. Electrical surge — the experimenting creature takes **11 (2d10) lightning damage** (DC 14 Dexterity save for half).
  3. A police robot is dispatched to investigate the interference (arrives 1 minute).
  4. All 8 androids' download is disrupted — they wake immediately, confused and hostile.

### AREA 12 — COLONIZATION HOUSING SUPPLIES

*Towering piles of crates and barrel-shaped containers of horn-like material fill this chamber. Narrow aisles wind their way through the stacks, disappearing deeper into the room.*

These crates contain prefabricated housing modules designed to serve as base camps for up to 100 colonists each, intended for surface deployment upon reaching the destination planet. Each crate requires a **violet card** to open. Worker robots were to have transported and assembled them on the new world's surface.

**Occupants.** Four **phase spiders** (see *Monster Manual*) live in the hold, webs strung between containers near the room's center. The webbing is not visible from the entrance — the party walks into it only when exploring the central aisles in earnest. At that point, all four phase spiders attack, trying to use the crates to box in prey.

**Treasure.** Caught in the webbing are the remains of a type one biological entity. Among its equipment:
- Its **battleaxe** (standard)
- An **inactive hologram emitter** (see Appendix C) — it thought it was pretty
- One crate (already opened) containing **six all-weather shelters** (see Appendix C) with fully charged power discs

### AREA 14 — CARGO HOLD ENTRANCE

*A massive door once barred entry to the area beyond, but the steel valve is now bulging and buckled. Broken storage containers lie smashed around and inside the broken door, forming a 10-foot-high barricade of twisted metal. A large gap at the top of the pile leads into whatever lies beyond. Flickering light is visible through the opening.*

The crash sent this hold's contents flying into its own door, bending it outward and partially blocking the entry. The room's sensors were damaged in the crash — the central computer has never dispatched repair robots here.

Climbing over the barricade of twisted containers is trivially easy but **noisy**. Unless the party explicitly states they are moving silently and succeeds on a **DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) group check**, the mutant umber hulk in Area 15A hears them and cannot be surprised.

### AREA 15 — WRECKED CARGO HOLD

*The contents of this large, high-ceilinged chamber are strewn about the floor. High piles of steel crates and barrel-shaped containers of horn-like material lie smashed and broken in towering heaps. Bent metal bars, torn fabric, broken devices of incomprehensible purpose, and other debris lie everywhere. A portion of the northeast wall is buckled, showing tremendous impact damage. The lighting here is intermittent — plunging into darkness briefly before blazing back to daylight conditions.*

Once a well-organized cargo hold, the crash destroyed it utterly. It is now a junkyard and the territory of a mutant umber hulk.

A **mutant two-headed umber hulk** (see Level IV Appendix B) has claimed this hold after succumbing to radiation exposure from the nearby Area 13. If the party made noise at Area 14, it is waiting in ambush among the debris. Otherwise it is in its den (Area 15A). **Show Handout #67.**

**Flickering Lights.** At the start of each combat round, roll **1d6**:
- **6:** Lights go out — total darkness until next check
- On subsequent dark rounds, **5–6:** lights remain off; **1–4:** lights return

While dark, creatures without darkvision cannot see the umber hulk and are immune to its Confusing Gaze and Scintillating Gaze traits. This cuts both ways — the umber hulk cannot be targeted by sight-based attacks either.

### AREA 15A — MUTANT UMBER HULK DEN

*A 20-foot-high, 40-foot-wide pile of strange rectangular blocks — each massive, like temple building stones, smooth and somewhat blue-colored like horn — lies here among the shattered debris. A tunnel has been hewn into the pile, forming a cave mouth some 10 feet wide and 8 feet high.*

The blocks are raw building material (500+ blocks, each 4×4×6 feet, weighing 200 pounds) that could be manufactured into various components. The umber hulk discovered it could burrow through them and excavated a "cave" roughly 20 feet in diameter inside the heap as its den.

The creature is rummaging through its junk bin if the party arrived stealthily.

**Treasure.** An open box in the den holds shiny trash that caught the umber hulk's many eyes. Mixed in:
- 15 **gem bearings** (100 gp each)
- Three **needler clips** (see Appendix C, Level I)
- Two **power discs**
- One **fragmentation grenade** (see Appendix C, Level I)

### AREA 17 — LAIR OF THE VAMPOIDS

*This area is dark and crowded with hulking metal boxes, each connected to a myriad of tubes, pipes, and oily-looking ropes. The floor is metal and pools of condensation have formed over the years. The drip of water echoes through the gloom. Narrow paths wind among the silent machinery. The entire room is cold — much colder than anywhere else on the ship.*

This room houses subsystems related to the biological entity production areas. It is ominous, and well-suited to the weird stellar creatures that have claimed it.

**Active Machinery.** As the party moves through, machines briefly come to life — rattling noises, dripping fluids, the occasional creak or clunk. These are harmless environmental effects from power fluctuations.

### AREA 17A — VAMPOID NEST

*Six curious leathery pods hang from the ceiling near the center of the room. Each is dark brown and teardrop-shaped, swaying slightly as if in a light breeze. A single spindly support rises from each bulb to clutch a pipe or strut overhead. A light layer of frost coats the floor and ceiling, but the pods are untouched.*

Six **vampoids** (see Appendix B) are dormant here, wings wrapped around their bodies, each clinging to the ceiling on one leg. They resemble alien cocoons.

**Scent of Blood.** If any party member is currently missing hit points, the vampoids smell it even in their dormant state and **immediately awaken**, hungry and alert. An uninjured party can pass through without disturbing them on a successful **DC 9 Dexterity (Stealth) check**.

**Development.** The vampoids are intelligent and aware of the ship's situation. If they suffer significant losses (3 or more defeated), the survivors flee this level and potentially escape to the outside world through the lower cargo bay exit. If they escape, rumors of strange vampiric attacks soon spread through the frontier — a new threat for the region independent of the pacifier robot.

**Treasure.** Each vampoid carries a **violet card**; one also has a **yellow card**. They have accumulated:
- A gold ring (500 gp)
- Six gems (1,000 gp each)
- A silver and diamond bracelet (2,500 gp)
- A blaster pistol with a **drained power disc** (see Appendix C, Level I)

### AREA 18 — GENETICISTS' QUARTERS

*Four beds, their covers stained dark, are positioned about this room. Narrow doors are set into the walls between the beds. A transparent table surrounded by ladle-shaped chairs rests near the entrance. Three skeletons lie on the floor — but no skulls are visible among the rag-dressed bones.*

Four scientists charged with maintaining the DNA banks lived here. During the plague, one of them developed a disturbing reverence for a specimen they had collected and began worshipping it as a malignant deity. He slew his colleagues and took their heads as offerings (see Area 19). Their headless bodies remain here undiscovered.

**DC 15 Intelligence (Medicine) check:** Reveals each body died from savage large-bladed weapon attacks; heads were likely taken post-mortem.

**The narrow doors** are personal lockers — personal clothing and possessions in poor condition.

**Treasure.** Three **yellow cards** in the lockers; four pieces of **jewelry** (1d6 × 100 gp each). A locked hard plastic case (opens with a yellow card) contains a **sample collection pistol** with four darts (see Appendix C).

### AREA 19 — MADMAN'S SHRINE

*DM Note: If the party caused a computer fire on Level I (Area 28, Minor Fire result), the cylinder described below may be empty — the death-drinker is already free. See Development.*

*This room is dominated by a 10-foot-tall, 4-foot-wide cylinder filled with turquoise liquid. Suspended within is a terrible monstrosity: a humanoid thing covered in sharply angled plates, swept-back spikes, and jutting teeth from an underslung jaw. Larger than a man, the creature has six limbs — two insectile legs and four arms, two of which protrude from its upper back and end with blade-like talons. A pair of barbed tails are coiled around the unmoving creature. Before the cylinder are three skulls surrounded by bowls of congealed yellow substance.*

The creature is a **death-drinker** (see Appendix B), the apex predator of a world of vicious alien beasts. It is currently in suspended animation. The three skulls are from the scientists in Area 18 — offerings from the mad worshipper. **Show Handout #68.**

A search of the room finds another skeleton — the madman himself — with a large crude sword beside it, fashioned to resemble the death-drinker's natural weaponry. The sword functions as a longsword.

The bowls contain congealed fat: crude candles that emit dim light in a 15-foot radius and smoke terribly when lit.

**The Stasis Cylinder:** AC 15, 20 HP, immunity to poison and psychic damage. Destroying it frees the death-drinker immediately — the creature emerges with **disadvantage on all attack rolls, saves, and ability checks for 2 rounds** while it orients, then acts normally. It is hungry, angry, and the party is its first meal.

**Development — If Already Freed (Level I computer fire):** The death-drinker became aware of the party **30 minutes after they first arrived on this level**. It has been stalking them since, using **Chameleon Camouflage** to remain hidden. It attacks isolated or weakened party members — waiting for them to be separated, distracted by another combat, or mid-rest before striking. It retreats if reduced to half HP, healing in a hidden location (Supply Rooms, Area 26) before resuming the hunt.

**Development — Cylinder Destroyed by Party:** As above, but with the 2-round disorientation penalty. If reduced to half HP before it recovers its wits, it flees and hides.

**Treasure.** On the madman's skeleton:
- A **violet card**
- A crude gold amulet shaped vaguely like the death-drinker (200 gp)

### AREA 20 — GIBBERING MADNESS

*DM Note: A successful DC 16 Wisdom (Perception) check outside the door detects a faint, incomprehensible conversation — like many people discussing something simultaneously.*

*This room contains a filthy bed and a high bench covered in scattered glass and metal tools. Three narrow doors are set side-by-side in the south wall. In the center of the room: two masses of churning amorphous flesh, their pale red bodies covered with countless eyes and jabbering mouths.*

A ship's xenobiologist kept alien biological samples in stasis cages here. The earthquake that exposed the ship caused the small stasis cages to fall from the workbench and shatter. The escaped organisms devoured each other until only two remained: a pair of **gibbering mouthers** (see *Monster Manual*). They attack anything they see.

**Door Lock Failure.** When the door opens, the color card lock sparks and becomes permanently stuck open — a worker robot must repair it (arrives 1d3 × 10 minutes later). The door cannot be closed manually.

**Development — If Not Contained.** If the gibbering mouthers are not defeated here, they range freely over the level. Each hour they are free, each gains **10 HP** (maximum 99 HP after 3.5 hours, at which point they are CR 3 — 700 XP each).

**Treasure.**
- A **yellow card** under the bed
- One locker contains a **silver full-body suit** that repels toxic chemicals and dangerous bacteria: the wearer has **advantage on all Constitution saves against poison damage and effects**. However, if the wearer takes **10 or more piercing or slashing damage** from a single hit, the suit tears and no longer functions.

### AREA 21 — KITCHEN

*Bare countertops with large plastic trenchers are present. Along one wall is a bench with eight metal boxes, each with a dark glass door and small metal selector squares beside it, and a short black cylinder atop each. Along another wall is a fine metal mesh with a rivet. Nearby is a long narrow door. A dark glass plate with four rivets is near the entrance.*

Everything non-bolted was looted long ago. The metal boxes are **food dispensers** (selector squares = menu buttons, black cylinder = temperature knob). The metal mesh with rivet is a **communications call box** (no longer functional). The dark plate is the **daily menus screen** (non-functional). The narrow door leads to a **refrigeration unit** with empty shelves.

**Dispensers.** Roll 1d6+1 to determine how many dispensers still function. Each active dispenser: **50% chance** of producing poisonous food (DC 15 Constitution save or poisoned until long rest; no damage). Non-poisonous food is nutritious and alien in character — the DM should describe it imaginatively.

### AREA 22 — DINING ROOM

*A feast hall for a dozen or more, now with displaced, overturned, jumbled furnishings. Eating utensils and plastic trenchers lie scattered. White bones can be glimpsed among the mess. Atop one of the tables is a gleaming suit of armor made from smooth plates and ribbed leather-like material.*

This mess hall served the xenobiologists and geneticists on this level. The furniture is jumbled and scattered with bones — nothing of value. The skeletons are unremarkable.

**The Armor.** The armor resembles **powered armor** (see Appendix C, Level I) but is a **mimic** (see *Monster Manual*) that has adopted this form to attract curious investigators — biological entities are interested in its appearance and make easy victims. The mimic attacks the moment anyone picks it up or places it on the table to examine it closely.

### AREA 23 — SEALED DROP TUBES

*A cylindrical chamber about 20 feet in diameter stands in the center of this area. Its interior is accessible by two open archways on opposite sides. Within the cylinder, a pair of wide bands bearing metal handles spaced 8 feet apart run vertically down each of the other two walls. The floor is solid, covered in tightly overlapping angled metal plates.*

When the spaceship was intact, these drop tubes led to additional decks below. When the ship broke apart from its lower sections, sphincter seals activated to preserve the atmosphere of the jettisoned section. Even if the seal were somehow breached, only solid rock lies underneath — the vessel is grounded. The drop tubes are unpowered and the handles are immobile. This room is a dead end.

### AREA 24 — LOOTED QUARTERS

*A room that may have once been living quarters, long disused. Furniture made from rusting metal and a now-dingy rigid material similar to leather. The state of the chamber suggests someone or something looted it thoroughly.*

These were living quarters for the level's scientific staff. Each contains jumbled furniture, rotting goods, and 1d4−1 inanimate skeletons. Everything is worthless or in bad condition. These rooms contain nothing of encounter or treasure value.

---

### AREA 25 — CARGO HOLDS

*A large space crammed with giant boxes and cylinders, each marked with indecipherable glyphs. Containers stacked near the ceiling, arranged with sense of order. Aisles pass through the stacked piles. Some containers appear damaged.*

These holds are still filled with crates and containers of **building materials, fertilizers, and colonization supplies**. Human bones are scattered throughout — remnants of the plague when crewmembers went insane. **Nothing of value** in any cargo hold not specially keyed.

---

### AREA 26 — SUPPLY ROOMS

*Bare shelves and litter — the room has been looted long ago.*

These supply rooms were pillaged during the plague. Nothing of value remains. They are ignored by all of the level's inhabitants **except the death-drinker** (see Area 19). If the death-drinker is still in stasis, a party can take a **long rest** in one of these rooms without random encounter risk.

### AREA 27 — AUTOMATED ASSEMBLY AREA

*Hulking metal machines of indeterminate purpose crowd this chamber. Long lengths of oily leather-like material extend on rollers passing through the devices. A walkway accessible by ladders runs along the chamber's perimeter, overlooking the silent machines.*

This automated factory was designed to assemble intricate mechanisms and equipment for deployment on the destination planet. The machines are powered down and ill-maintained. A **behir** recently released from stasis has claimed this area as its den, coming and going through the damaged western door (permanently open).

**Behir Tactics.** The behir hides among the machinery. It attempts to constrict and swallow isolated party members before the rest can respond. Use standard **behir statistics** (*Monster Manual*).

**Lightning Surge Development.** Any creature struck by the behir's **Lightning Breath** must also make a **DC 15 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check**. On a failure, a section of assembly line machinery springs to life from the power surge — the creature is **restrained** (escape DC 12) by robotic assembly arms seizing its clothing, limbs, or equipment. A restrained creature takes **9 (2d8) bludgeoning damage** at the end of each of its turns until it escapes.

The behir's breath activates a 10-square-foot section of equipment each time it fires — multiple surges can restrain multiple creatures simultaneously.

## DM NOTES: USING LEVEL VII

**The pacifier robot is the adventure's resolution.** Everything in the module's exterior (the raids, the baron's desperation, the crashed ship investigation) traces back to Area 1. The party doesn't need to fight the robot directly — operating the monitoring station is enough. Emphasize that this level exists to explain and conclude the threat, not just to be another dungeon floor.

**The death-drinker changes the level's tension entirely.** If it's already loose, the level becomes a psychological horror gauntlet — the party knows something is stalking them, they can hear its approach, but their lights make it harder to see. The Chameleon Camouflage means it will almost always get its Ambusher advantage. Consider telegraphing its presence with subtle signs: something moved in the peripheral vision, a faint disturbed condensation trail, a brief smell of ozone.

**Biological entity production sabotage.** Destroying all 24 vats across Areas 2 and 3 (1,200 XP bonus) and all 167 android blanks in Area 10 permanently cripples the ship's capacity to produce new soldiers. This is a meaningful strategic victory even if the pacifier robot is still active.

**Vampoid aftermath.** If the vampoids escape to the outside world, this creates a lasting consequence — a new vampire-like predator threat in the frontier that future adventures can address. The DM should decide whether this is a loose end worth following or a consequence to wave away.

**The inert pacifier robot.** If the party somehow activates the docked unit (they cannot — the text says nothing they do can activate it without computer authorization), it would be the most dangerous thing in the module at CR 11. The DM may want to keep this in mind if the party decides to get creative.

**Connecting to Chapter 5.** If using the Crash Site Environs (Chapter 5), the pacifier robot is currently at the invaders' encampment (Area 1-6 in the exterior). If recalled via the monitoring station, the DM should describe the robot breaking off mid-patrol and returning, leaving the biological entity troops leaderless.
