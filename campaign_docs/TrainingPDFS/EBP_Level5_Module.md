# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level V — Service Deck 6 / Garden 'Tween Decks
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Connects to:** Level IV above (trapdoors marked "T" on map; brown card required from below | concealed "S" hatches throughout Level IV flagstone circles), Level VI below (sloping passageway via Area 22 trapper location; drop tube terminus)  
**Primary Threats:** A greater slithering tracker lurking in Area 1's pool; six gray oozes disguised as damp flooring in Area 3; radiation in Area 13; a malfunctioning wheely sled that locks into a runaway death charge  
**Tone:** Claustrophobic, industrial, and wet. A pitch-black maintenance crawlspace beneath a living garden. Dripping pipes. Colorful harmless mold on every surface. The humidity is oppressive. This is where crew worked out of sight — and where a few things found the dark useful.

---

## OVERVIEW

**Show Handout #17** the moment the party enters this level from any access point.

Level V is the service infrastructure layer beneath the Level IV botanical garden. It was used primarily for water circulation machinery, purification systems, and irrigation controls for the garden deck above. After the plague, the robots mostly ignored it. Most of the machinery here still runs — pipes still carry water, filters still hum — but maintenance has been absent for decades, and leaks are everywhere.

This is not a destination. It is a passage. Parties who enter from Level IV are likely looking for a route to Level VI, or were dropped here by a collapsing concealed hatch. Parties who enter from Level VI below are cutting upward through the ship.

The level has only a handful of keyed areas. The rest is a labyrinth of low service corridors, machinery alcoves, junction boxes, and crawl spaces — all marked on the level map but empty of encounters.

---

## GENERAL FEATURES

**Total Darkness.** All 'tween decks levels are completely unlit. There are no emergency lights, no bioluminescence, no active technology providing illumination. The party must provide all light sources. Note that light sources in the 'tween decks do not trigger gasbat reactions — the gasbats are confined to Level IV above.

**Ceiling Height.** 7 feet in main corridors. 5 feet in service crawlways (marked on map). Medium-sized creatures must squeeze through crawlways (half speed, disadvantage on attack rolls and Dexterity saving throws). Large creatures cannot enter crawlways.

**Splotch-Marked Areas.** Irregular patches of harmless mold — yellowish, greenish, pinkish, and bluish — cover virtually every surface. These vivid growths thrive in the humid conditions from the leaking water filtration systems. They are completely harmless and non-toxic, but they make every surface look potentially dangerous to a paranoid party.

**Solid Areas.** Several sections of the level map are filled with solid material — continuations of the structural supports for Level IV above. These cannot be entered. The walls encasing these areas are plasteel (AC 20, immune to fire and cold damage, and to nonmagical piercing and slashing).

**Eastern Condensation Zone.** The entire eastern section of the level — from the edge of the lake's bulkhead to the ship's hull — is coated in heavy condensation. Every surface drips. Small puddles cover the floor throughout this zone.

Any creature that takes the **Dash action** in the condensation zone must make a **DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check**:
- **Success:** No effect.
- **Failure:** Creature slips and falls, then makes a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw**:
  - **Success:** Knocked prone; no damage.
  - **Failure:** Knocked prone and **stunned for 1 minute**. (Can repeat Con save at end of each turn.)
  - **Failure by 10 or more (roll of 5 or less):** Knocked prone, takes **3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage**, and falls **unconscious for 30 minutes**. Another creature can use an action to revive the unconscious creature.

**Trapdoors.** Ceiling hatches (marked "T" on the map) are entryways to Level IV above. From this side (Level V), they require a **brown color card** to operate. From Level IV, they are concealed under foliage and require a DC 15 Perception check to find — or are located by referencing the Level IV general features (circular flagstone resting areas marked "S").

**Unkeyed Mechanical Spaces (Areas 6–12).** The numbered spaces between Areas 5 and 13 on the map are machinery alcoves, junction rooms, empty storage bays, and service crawlways. None contain encounters. A party searching these spaces thoroughly finds nothing of value — only pipes, valves, corroded machinery, and more colorful mold. Exploration of one unkeyed area takes about 10 minutes.

---

## WANDERING MONSTERS

There are no wandering monsters on this level. The greater slithering tracker in Area 1 is the apex predator here; other creatures avoid its territory. The gray oozes in Area 3 do not wander. The radiation in Area 13 prevents organic creatures from lingering.

## ENCOUNTER AREAS

---

### AREA 1 — SHALLOW POOL

*The sound of steadily dripping water is loud here. Water leaks from a pipe overhead, creating a pool some 50 feet wide and 30 feet across at its broadest. The pool drains eastward in a narrow rivulet. Patches of colorful fungi in hues of yellow, green, pink, and blue thrive along the pool's shores.*

A stream of water from a nearby pipe and overhead dripping have formed a shallow pool that drains slowly eastward to the drop tube terminus. The water is **2–6 inches deep** except near the drain terminus where it reaches **1 foot**.

In the western third of the pool dwells a **greater slithering tracker** (see Appendix B). This 6-foot-long transparent creature lies absolutely still, indistinguishable from the shallow water around it. It will strike like a snake at all who enter the pool, attempting to grapple and drain as much prey as possible before retreating.

**Tactics.** The slithering tracker enters combat by surging from concealment to strike the foremost party member entering the pool. It targets whoever appears most isolated, attempting to grapple with Life Leech and hold on while its Damage Transfer trait halves the damage returned to it. It will not pursue out of the water; if driven back, it sinks to the bottom and waits.

**Detecting It.** A creature actively studying the pool before entering can spot the slithering tracker with a **DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) check**. Without active investigation, it is automatically undetected until it attacks.

---

### AREA 2 — YELLOW MOLD PATCHES

*Splotches of vivid yellow growth cling to the low ceiling overhead in this section of corridor. They look similar to all the other colorful mold on this level — but something about this particular variety has an unpleasant, musty edge to the smell.*

Each of these patches is **yellow mold** (*Dungeon Master's Guide* p. 105), not the harmless decorative variety found elsewhere on the level. The yellow mold clings to the ceiling.

Any creature standing **5½ feet or taller** must succeed on a **DC 8 Dexterity saving throw** when passing beneath a yellow mold patch or brush against it, triggering a cloud of spores. A creature that **crawls** automatically avoids contact and need not roll.

**Yellow Mold (DMG hazard).** If disturbed, the mold ejects a cloud of spores in a 10-foot cube. Each creature in the area must succeed on a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** or take **11 (2d10) poison damage** and be **poisoned** until it spends 1 minute without contact with more yellow mold. Dead or organic matter that touches yellow mold becomes covered in the mold. Yellow mold is destroyed by fire; a torch held to it for 1 round kills a single patch.

**DM Note.** The yellow mold patches are clustered in this area — any narrow corridor section here forces all Medium characters to duck or risk contact. Parties who established torchlight can easily destroy all patches. Parties relying on darkvision may not recognize them as different from the harmless decorative mold until the first spore cloud goes off.

---

### AREA 3 — "DAMP" FLOOR

*A long patch of fungi sprouts here to create a 40-foot-long, 10-foot-wide "finger" of colorful growth on the metal floor. The floor appears much damper around the growth than elsewhere, suggesting the fungi are thriving in a moist area.*

This is the most dangerous area on the level for the unwary. The "damp floor" patches on either side of the fungi strip are not condensation — they are **gray oozes**. There are **three oozes to the west** of the fungi and **three oozes to the east**, six total.

The oozes use the standard gray ooze statistics (*Monster Manual*). The colorful fungi strip down the center is genuine and harmless, but it serves as camouflage, creating a visual texture that makes the oozes on either side even harder to distinguish from wet floor.

**Detection.** A creature actively scrutinizing the floor before walking on it can detect an ooze with a **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check**. Moving at normal speed without inspection: no check — the ooze simply attacks when stepped on.

**Tactics.** The oozes attack individually as creatures enter their space. They attempt to corrode metal equipment and grapple any creature that enters their space. They do not pursue beyond their current positions.

**Note on the Fungi.** The colorful fungal strip down the center is genuine — edible, harmless, and actually somewhat pleasant-smelling. A curious party member who tastes it will find it earthy and not unpleasant.

---

### AREA 4 — WHEELY SLED

*An intriguing-looking conveyance is parked here, resting atop four black wheels largely obscured by metal sheaths. It is the size of a small cart but lacks any sort of yoke or handle.*

**Show Handout #18.** This is a wheely sled (see Level II, Area 3 for full vehicle rules). However, this particular unit has a **catastrophic malfunction** in its steering and acceleration controls.

**The Malfunction.** Once driven for 1 round, the vehicle's acceleration locks to maximum and its steering mechanism seizes — no turns are possible. The sled accelerates to its maximum speed and holds it until it strikes a solid obstruction, at which point it crashes and is destroyed.

**Runaway Crash.** When the sled enters a wall or large obstacle at full speed:
- All riders who have not already bailed out must make a **DC 16 Dexterity saving throw**
- **Failure:** **21 (6d6) bludgeoning damage** and knocked prone
- **Success:** Half damage, not knocked prone
- The wheely sled is **irreparably destroyed** in the crash

**Bailing Out.** A rider can bail out on any of their turns before the crash as an action. They land prone in the nearest unoccupied space and take **7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage** from the tumble (no save). Bailing at full speed is unpleasant; bailing before the sled fully accelerates (first round) deals only **3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage**.

**DM Note.** The sled's malfunction is apparent only once it is activated. A successful **DC 14 Intelligence check** while inspecting the vehicle reveals corrosion and damage to the steering column suggesting mechanical trouble — but not precisely what will happen.

### AREA 5 — REPAIR ROBOT FACILITY

*This smaller chamber contains two of the bizarre metal duck-billed creatures that inhabit the complex. They slump motionless against one wall of the room, their metal bodies opened and displaying a perplexing amount of colorful string and thin pieces of horn inside.*

Two **worker robots** (see Appendix B, Level I module) are present here in a state of complete disrepair. **They cannot be restored** to proper working order by any efforts the party can attempt — the internal damage is too extensive and the required replacement parts do not exist on this level.

**Treasure.** Careful examination of the second worker robot — a **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check** or 5 minutes of deliberate inspection — reveals an **atmosphere analyzer** (see Appendix C, Level IV module) bolted to its exterior case. It can be removed without tools in about 1 minute, and it is **functional** (unlike the 30% chance for the one in the froghemoth's nest on Level IV).

**Robot Contents.** The "colorful string" visible inside the open robot chassis is a tangle of optical fiber cabling and polymer wiring. The "thin pieces of horn" are circuit boards — alien computing substrates of organic material pressed flat. None of it has value to the party beyond curiosity.

### AREAS 6–12 — UNKEYED MECHANICAL SPACES

These seven areas marked on the Level V map are service infrastructure rooms — pipe junctions, pressure regulators, water treatment filters, and the like. All are in various states of disrepair; all are dark, dripping, and covered in the level's characteristic colorful mold.

Searching each area takes approximately **10 minutes** and finds nothing of encounter value. Any one of these areas can serve as a safe resting location — no creatures hunt here, and the area is completely enclosed. However, the constant dripping, close quarters, and oppressive humidity impose a **one level of exhaustion** cost on any character who attempts a short rest without light, due to the psychological strain of complete darkness in a deteriorating crawlspace. (A light source eliminates this cost.)

---

### AREA 13 — RADIATION AREAS

*A faint green glow pulses from within these rooms, as if everything inside is under some eerie magical effect. The air tastes metallic and wrong.*

These rooms contain active radiation sources — contaminants from corroded power systems, unstable fuel cells, and machinery that has been slowly leaking radioactive material since the plague. The glow is genuine ionizing radiation, not an illusion.

**Entering a Radiation Area.** Any creature capable of being poisoned that enters one of these rooms is immediately at risk. After **(1d4 + 1) × 10 minutes** of cumulative exposure, the creature must succeed on a **DC 10 Constitution saving throw** or become **poisoned** by radiation sickness.

**Radiation Sickness Progression.**
- Immediately upon becoming poisoned: no other effect
- After **1 hour** poisoned: creature loses **1 point of Strength**
- After each subsequent hour while still poisoned: loses another **1 point of Strength**
- At **0 Strength**: creature dies

**Removing Radiation Sickness.** An **anti-radiation serum** (see Appendix C, Level I module) immediately removes the poisoned condition and ends all Strength loss. Lost Strength is regained with a **long rest** after the serum is administered. *Greater restoration* also ends the condition.

**Powered Armor.** A character wearing powered armor (see Appendix C, Level I module) is fully protected from radiation damage — the suit's atmosphere analysis equipment detects the hazard and its sealed environment prevents exposure.

**What's In There.** The rooms contain corroded machinery, cracked containment vessels, and pools of fluorescent liquid that drains slowly into structural seams. Nothing of value is recoverable — even if an object were worth taking, the radiation contamination would make it dangerous to carry without proper containment. The atmosphere analyzer (if functional) will read these rooms as EXTREME RADIATION HAZARD and emit an audible alarm.

### GREATER SLITHERING TRACKER
*Medium ooze, chaotic evil*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Armor Class** | 14 |
| **Hit Points** | 67 (9d8 + 27) |
| **Speed** | 30 ft., climb 30 ft., swim 30 ft. |
| **Initiative** | +4 |

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 16 (+3) | 19 (+4) | 16 (+3) | 10 (+0) | 14 (+2) | 11 (+0) |

**Proficiency Bonus:** +2  
**Skills:** Stealth +8  
**Damage Vulnerabilities:** Cold, fire  
**Damage Resistances:** Bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks  
**Condition Immunities:** Blinded, deafened, exhaustion, grappled, paralyzed, petrified, prone, restrained, unconscious  
**Senses:** Blindsight 120 ft., passive Perception 12  
**Languages:** Understands languages it knew in its previous form, but cannot speak  
**Challenge:** 4 (1,100 XP)

**Ambusher.** In the first round of combat, the slithering tracker has advantage on attack rolls against any creature it surprised.

**Damage Transfer.** While grappling a creature, the slithering tracker takes only half the damage dealt to it (rounded down), and the creature it is grappling takes the other half.

**False Appearance.** While the slithering tracker remains motionless, it is indistinguishable from a puddle of water or shallow pool unless an observer succeeds on a **DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) check**.

**Keen Tracker.** The slithering tracker has advantage on Wisdom checks to track prey.

**Liquid Form.** The slithering tracker can enter an enemy's space and stop there. It can also move through a space as narrow as 1 inch wide without squeezing.

**Spider Climb.** The slithering tracker can climb difficult surfaces, including upside down on ceilings, without needing to make an ability check.

**Watery Stealth.** While underwater, the slithering tracker has advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made to hide, and it can take the Hide action as a bonus action.

**ACTIONS**

**Slam.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 8 (1d10 + 3) bludgeoning damage.

**Life Leech.** One Large or smaller creature the slithering tracker can see within 5 feet must succeed on a **DC 13 Dexterity saving throw** or be **grappled** (escape DC 13). While grappled this way:
- The target is **restrained**
- At the start of each of the slithering tracker's turns, the target takes **9 (2d8) necrotic damage** and must succeed on a **DC 13 Constitution saving throw** or gain **1 level of exhaustion**
- The grapple ends if the slithering tracker moves more than 5 feet from the target or uses Life Leech on a different creature

The slithering tracker can have only one creature grappled via Life Leech at a time.

*A 6-foot-long, transparent length of living liquid that whips its tail like a frenzied tadpole. It has no eyes, mouth, or facial features. It was once a living creature — a victim of the ship's alien biology who was consumed and transformed by the slithering tracker's progenitor. Its intelligence persists as something dark and hunting.*

## DM NOTES: USING LEVEL V

**As a transit level.** Most parties will pass through Level V quickly, entering from a Level IV "S" hatch or trapper-revealed entrance and exiting through the sloping passage to Level VI or a drop tube. The primary hazards are the slithering tracker (Area 1, likely the entry point from the pool-adjacent hatch), the oozes (Area 3, a serious threat if not detected), and the radiation rooms (Area 13, most dangerous if unrecognized).

**Pacing.** This level should feel short and tense — a gauntlet of hazards in a dark, dripping crawlspace, not an extended exploration. The goal is to get through, find what's here, and keep moving.

**The wheely sled as a running gag.** If the party found the functional wheely sled on Level II and used it extensively, the Level V version serves as a dark callback — same vehicle, same charm, catastrophically broken. Experienced drivers will recognize the vehicle and may assume it works. Let them discover the problem naturally.

**Radiation map display.** If the party later reaches Level VI and accesses the Chief's Office computer console (Area 42B on Level VI), the console displays all radiation zones on the ship's level maps — pointing to these Area 13 rooms, the radiation zones on Level I, and others. If the party has already explored Level V, they can confirm the display against their experience.
