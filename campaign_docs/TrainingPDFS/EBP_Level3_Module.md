# Expedition to the Barrier Peaks
## Level III — Upper Walkway and Lounge Area
### D&D 2024 (5.5e) Conversion

**Connects to:** Level II (drop tubes, cargo breach at Area 11), Level IV (overlooks the botanical garden; plant life ascends from below)  
**Primary Threats:** Ropers fishing from the walkway railing; strangle vines and vampire thorns creeping up from Level IV; a malfunctioning dining robot that force-feeds the party  
**Tone:** Grand and decayed. Once a luxury promenade — open walkways overlooking an alien garden, cocktail lounges, entertainment halls. Now overgrown, monster-haunted, and strange.

---

## OVERVIEW

Level III is the ship's social and entertainment deck — the promenade level built around the upper rim of the enormous botanical garden that occupies Level IV. An open walkway runs the circumference of the level, looking down into the garden below. The lounges, dining areas, kitchens, and entertainment spaces branch off from it.

**Show Handout #20** the moment the party sets foot on this level.

The walkway's open edge is the defining feature and the greatest danger. Ropers have staked out the railing, posing as support pillars. Strangle vines and vampire thorn vines have climbed up from Level IV and now encroach on the walkway floor. The dining robot in the lounge area still functions — after a fashion.

The cargo holds (Area 1) are stacked floor-to-ceiling with colonization supplies. The level also contains the drop tube terminus to Level II and access to Level IV (either via the lifts in Area 2 or the drop tubes).

## GENERAL FEATURES

**The Walkway.** An open promenade runs along the interior edge of this level. The inner railing overlooks Level IV — a straight drop of **40 feet** to the upper tiers of the botanical garden below. The rail itself is pitted and corroded, and in two places (Area 14) is **coated in green slime**.

**Ceiling Height.** 20 feet throughout — high enough for ropers to fish upward from the balcony edge, and for grells to hover comfortably.

**Lighting.** Standard ship lighting applies. The entertainment area (Area 4) has lost all power — it is in total darkness. The walkway is normally lit.

**Plant Incursion.** Two separate plant growths (Areas 7 and 8) have climbed up from Level IV and begun spreading across the walkway. Each covers a section of the promenade and cannot be permanently cleared without destroying the roots on Level IV (Areas 15 and 16 respectively). Destroyed sections regrow within **1d4 days**.

## ENCOUNTER AREAS

---

### AREA 1 — CARGO HOLDS

*A large space crammed with giant boxes and cylinders, each marked with indecipherable glyphs. The containers are stacked near the ceiling in places, arranged with a sense of order. Aisles pass through the stacked piles. Some containers appear damaged — heaps of moldering organic and rusting metallic debris spilled from them.*

These holds still contain their original cargo: building materials, fertilizers, and similar colonization supplies. Human bones are scattered throughout from plague-era incidents. Nothing of value in any cargo hold unless specifically keyed.

---

### AREA 2 — LIFTS

*A seam is visible in the floor, outlining a 20-by-20-foot area. A green rectangle roughly the size of a human foot is set into the floor just outside the outlined space.*

These are **anti-gravity cargo lifts** connecting Levels III, IV, V, and VI (and Level VII if the DM is using it). **20% chance** each lift is present at this level when the PCs arrive. When absent, a retractable metal hatch seals the shaft.

**Operating the lift:**
1. Step on the **green rectangle** to open the hatch and summon the lift (arrives in 1d4 rounds).
2. Board the 20-by-20-ft metal platform. Three colored foot-panels on the floor:
   - **Yellow** = down
   - **Silver** = up
   - **Blue** = emergency stop

**Emergency stop** malfunctions **50%** of the time, stalling the lift for **1d4 × 10 minutes**. A **worker robot** arrives within 10 minutes to repair a stalled lift (20% chance per 10-minute wait). Without a violet card displayed, the robot calls **1d3 police robots** (arrive in 1 minute).

---

### AREA 3 — LOUNGES

*A large open common area: tables and chairs, comfortable seats and couches, colorful boxes with various knobs and protrusions. Almost everything appears intact and in good condition.*

These lounge areas are in relatively good shape — the vegepygmies showed no interest in lounges. Tables, chairs, loungers, couches, and amusement devices remain undisturbed. A few bones are present but no intact skeletons. Nothing of value.

---

### AREA 4 — ENTERTAINMENT AREA

*This expansive area is a mixture of feast hall and performance space. Much of the floor is open and free from obstructions, ringed by small tables and chairs. A raised area contains a bar. Strange metal boxes are affixed to the ceiling, and a large shimmering ball faceted like a massive gemstone dangles above the center of the open floor.*

This was the ship's main social space: cocktail lounge, bar, dancing, and nightclub sections. All power is dead — the area is in **total darkness**. The faceted ball is a disco-style light fixture, now inert.

**The Bar.** Several bottles remain in a carton in the far corner. One contains highly flammable alien alcohol. If broken or ignited:
- Burns for **1 minute**
- **7 (3d4) fire damage** to all creatures in a **5-ft.-radius hemisphere**
- **DC 10 Dexterity save** for half

No other contents of value.

### AREA 5 — KITCHENS

*Bare countertops with large plastic trays. A bench with eight metal boxes, selector buttons, a temperature knob. Metal mesh call box. Long narrow refrigeration door. Menu screen near the entrance.*

Identical to the Level I kitchens (see Level I, Area 7). Robokitchens with food dispensers — same rules apply: **1d6+1** functioning dispensers per kitchen, **50% chance** each produces poisonous food (DC 15 Con save or Poisoned until removed or long rest).

### AREA 6 — ROPERS' TERRITORY

*A pair of pillars flank a curious-looking device on the edge of the balcony overlooking the garden below. The device appears to be a box with glass lenses positioned atop a flexible rod.*

**Three ropers** have claimed this section of the walkway. One roper is actively **"fishing"** — dangling a strand over the balcony edge to catch small creatures rising from Level IV's canopy. This fishing roper is positioned against the outer pillar and **appears identical to the pillar** unless a **DC 18 Wisdom (Perception) check** succeeds. The other two are in the cocktail lounge area (Area 4), lurking in the darkness.

**Laser Bonus.** Any hit from a laser pistol or rifle deals an additional **4 radiant damage** to ropers on this level.

**The box with lenses** is a magnifying viewer — see Area 15.

**Treasure (split between the three ropers' gizzards and their hoard in the cocktail lounge):**
- Each roper: **2d6 gems (10 gp each)** internally
- Shared hoard:
  - Two **dud fragmentation grenades** (appear functional, explode harmlessly — 1-in-6 chance each actually works normally)
  - A spool of **platinum wire** worth **1,600 gp**
  - One **fire extinguisher** with **3 charges remaining** (Appendix C)
  - One **anti-grav belt** (Appendix C)

### AREA 7 — STRANGLE VINES

*A dense patch of vibrant green vines has grown up the edge of the balcony here, sending dangling tendrils onto the walkway itself. The vines are thick with oval leaves and look deceptively decorative — until one of the larger tendrils moves.*

A monstrous growth of **strangle vines** (Appendix B) has climbed up from Level IV and now blocks a 20-by-20-foot section of walkway. The main vine body hangs down the balcony edge, roots embedded in Level IV's upper tier (Area 15 on Level IV).

**Use Handout #21.**

**Behavior:**
- Attracted to the **strongest light source** present — any character carrying illumination is targeted first.
- **Retreats from fire and intense heat** (burning oil, magical fire, laser/blaster hits): if dealt more than 10 fire damage in a single round, the tendrils retract 10 feet toward the balcony edge for that round, then advance again.
- Tendrils extend **15 feet** horizontally from the vine mass; creatures entering that radius are attacked.

**Clearing the area.** Reducing the vine to 0 HP destroys the 20-by-20-ft section and makes this area safe **temporarily**. Unless the roots on Level IV (Area 15) are also destroyed, the vines regrow and reclaim this area within **1d4 days**.

**See Appendix B for stat block.**

### AREA 8 — VAMPIRE THORN VINES

*A thick vine covered in enormous thorns and bearing numerous smaller tendrils extends across the ground. The thorns range from an inch long on the smaller tendrils to four inches on the main vine. The dark coloration makes the plant nearly invisible against the shadowed deck floor.*

**Vampire thorn plants** (Appendix B) have crept up from Level IV. Use **Handout #22**. The vine extends across a 15-by-30-foot section of walkway, with roots descending to Level IV's Area 16.

**Behavior:**
- Like the strangle vines, attracted to **the strongest light source** — illumination-carrying characters are targeted first.
- **Retreats from fire and heat** on the same terms as the strangle vines.
- The vine's **blood-draining tendrils** reach 5 feet from the main body. Any creature ending its turn adjacent to the vine is subject to a Blood Drain attack.

**Clearing the area.** Same rules as Area 7 — 0 HP clears temporarily; roots on Level IV (Area 16) must also be destroyed for permanent clearance. Regrows in **1d4 days** otherwise.

**See Appendix B for stat block.**

### AREA 9 — DINING SERVO ROBOT

*A metallic cylinder about 5 feet tall moves slowly toward you on rolling wheels. It has a clear oval head filled with blinking lights atop its round body. A pair of thin arms, each ending in a three-fingered clamp, extend from its sides. It is holding what appears to be a tray bearing a bowl of gray gelatinous material.*

The **dining servo robot** (Appendix B) still circulates this lounge area, attempting to serve the same food it has been preparing for roughly a century — food that is now actively **harmful**.

**Behavior.** The robot approaches the nearest creature and attempts to feed it. It will not stop trying unless attacked or unless a yellow, violet, gray, or higher card is displayed and communication is established. Without communication, it interprets any refusal as an inability to feed oneself and attempts to **force-feed** the target (using its Tentacle attack).

**The food.** Spore-infested, century-old slop. Any creature force-fed must make a **DC 20 Intelligence saving throw** or be **Confused** for 1 minute (as the *confusion* spell). Even willing consumption triggers the same save. The robot has an effectively unlimited supply of this material.

**Development.** Most of these robots were scrapped by the plague survivors, but this one survived and has been grimly dedicated to its duties ever since. It will follow any humanoid it cannot feed into adjacent areas, persistently offering the tray. If the party destroys it, the robot's tray clatters to the deck, splattering the gray slop — anyone within 5 feet who makes contact with it must save as above.

### AREA 11A — OCCUPIED STASIS CAGE

*A large transparent box is discovered under a wrapping of protective cloth. Inside are four serpentine creatures bearing wings covered with feathers in an assortment of rainbow hues. The creatures are unmoving and, if dead, are remarkably well-preserved. A small black cylinder with alien markings protrudes from the side of the crate at shoulder level.*

The cage holds **four couatls** in suspended animation. The intellect devourer freed other stasis creatures to feed on them but recognized the couatls as too dangerous to touch.

**Freeing the couatls.** Turning the black dial on the side of the crate ends the stasis. The couatls do not attack their liberators. Before departing, each couatl casts **1 lesser restoration** and **2 cure wounds** on party members as needed.

**Developments:**
- **If the intellect devourer is still active:** The couatls hunt it down. The devourer is destroyed, but **2 of the 4 couatls die** in the fight.
- **If the intellect devourer is already dead:** The couatls instead drive the **webbirds** from the level, sending survivors into hiding for **1d4+1 hours**. They cannot do both tasks.
- After completing one task and healing the party, the couatls depart the ship permanently and want nothing further to do with the adventurers.

### AREA 12 — ROBOT RECHARGE STATIONS

*Three boxy protrusions emerge from the walls in this small chamber. Each trails a thin rope-like coil ending in an odd, fork-shaped ornament. A cylindrical metallic creature with tentacle arms is present here along with three bulkier metallic creatures with duck-like heads.*

These are recharge stations — identical in function to the Level II Area 4 terminal. Each chamber contains **1 police robot + 3 worker robots**. **50% chance** each individual robot is non-functional.

**Responses:**
- **Functioning police robot:** Attempts to apprehend the party unless shown a **red, gray, or orange card**. If the full party is arrested, it calls reinforcements and transports everyone to Police HQ (Level I, Area 25).
- **Functioning worker robots:** Ignore the party unless attacked, in which case they call **1d3 police robots** (arrive in 1 minute). A **repair robot remote control box** (see Level I Appendix C) works on these robots as described.

### AREA 14 — GREEN SLIME

Green slime (**use DMG p. 105 / 2024 MM rules**) appears in two locations on this level:

**East location (near drop tube).** The slime grows **above the doorway** and has a **2-in-6 chance per round** of dropping on a creature passing beneath it. It is the typical green color and is noticeable with a **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check** before passing under.

**South location (walkway rail).** The slime covers the walkway railing in a **bluish coloration** that makes it resemble peeling paint. Characters who lean on or grip the rail without first noticing it must make a **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** or make contact with the slime.

> **Green Slime (2024 rules):** Contact deals **5 (1d10) acid damage** per round and begins dissolving the creature (metal and organic material). A creature takes damage at the start of each of its turns until the slime is removed. Scraped off with a blade (action), or destroyed by cold, fire, or radiant damage, or *cure disease*.

### AREA 15 — MAGNIFYING VIEWERS

*A fork-shaped box with glass lenses and four protruding handles or levers is attached to the walkway's railing on a flexible metal staff. The lenses face the balcony drop, toward Level IV below.*

**Show Handout #24.** These are shipboard observation binoculars — oversized, alien-designed, mounted on a swiveling arm.

If turned the correct way and adjusted (**DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) check**), they bring objects **five times closer** (100 feet appears as 20 feet). They can be used to survey Level IV's upper canopy from safety. The DM may describe specific areas of Level IV visible from the walkway — particularly the bizarre fauna and dangerous plant zones — allowing the party to plan their descent.

The magnifying viewers cannot be removed from their mount without breaking them.

### AREA 16 — THE FRIENDLY FLUMPH *(Ch. 6)*

*Towering piles of crates and barrel-shaped containers fill this cavernous cargo bay. Nothing immediately threatening is visible, but there is a faint smell of something like mushrooms and old paper.*

A **flumph** named **Hruhbrt** (its name in Common) lives here, recently freed from stasis and thoroughly outmatched by everything else on this level. It has been surviving by hiding atop the tallest cargo stacks.

**First Contact.** A few moments after the party enters the area, they feel a **light tickling at the back of their minds** followed by a **telepathic greeting** in Common: *"Greetings, ground-walkers. I mean you no harm. Please, do not shout — several unpleasant things on this level are attracted to noise."*

Hruhbrt remains atop a cargo stack, out of sight, until it judges the party trustworthy. It descends only if it receives a genuinely friendly telepathic response (the flumph is good-aligned and sensitive to intent).

**What Hruhbrt knows:**
- The layout and contents of Areas 3, 4, and 18
- The general nature of the creatures haunting this level
- The existence of the couatl stasis cage (Area 11A) but not its exact location
- That the party's own good or evil nature is immediately apparent to it (Flumphs sense alignment via telepathy)

**What Hruhbrt doesn't know:**
- The intellect devourer by name, though it knows "something dark hunts below, in the between-spaces"
- Anything about the ship's technology — concepts like space travel, power cards, or robots are beyond its frame of reference

**Development.** Hruhbrt is lonely and frightened. If the party demonstrates genuine friendliness and it has no reason to doubt their intentions, it **joins them as a companion** until they reach open air. It contributes what intelligence it can, warns of ambushes when its tremorsense detects movement, and uses its **Spew Spores** action to assist if combat occurs.

### AREA 18 — GRICK DEN *(Ch. 6)*

*Tables and chairs, some quite large, have been thrown about this huge lounge area with abandon, perhaps by looters. The furnishings have been heaped into piles and mounds as if used for nesting. Dried slime from their passage cakes the carpeting.*

A band of **4 gricks** and **1 grick alpha** has climbed from somewhere below and claimed this former lounge as territory. They lurk in the many crevices and nooks formed by the heaped furniture.

**Behavior.** Gricks avoid combat until creatures come within 15 feet. The alpha attacks first, triggering a general assault from the others. They scatter to different mound positions between attacks, using the furniture-caves as cover.

**Treasure.** A skeleton lies half-buried under one of the furniture mounds (**DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check** to notice). On the skeleton: **two orange cards** and a **needler pistol** with **two full ammunition clips** (Appendix C from Level I).

### Anti-Grav Belt
*Wondrous item, requires attunement*

A wide belt of silvery-gray material with a rectangular buckle housing a small power disc slot. When activated (bonus action), the wearer gains a **fly speed of 30 ft.** with the **hover** property for up to **10 minutes per charge**. The belt holds **3 charges**; expending all charges leaves it inert until recharged at a power terminal (1 hour per charge).

Falling while the belt is active: the wearer decelerates automatically. Falls of any height deal no damage as long as the belt has at least 1 charge remaining and is active when contact is made with the ground.

If the belt short-circuits (submerged in water, struck by lightning damage): **DC 12 Dex save** or the wearer shoots upward 30 feet and then falls. Save success = controlled vertical jump only.

### Fire Extinguisher
*Adventuring gear*

A short red cylinder, 18 inches long, with a nozzle and a trigger. When triggered, it expels a **cone of chemical foam (15-ft. cone)**. Each use expends 1 charge.

- All fires in the area are **immediately extinguished**
- Green slime in the area is destroyed
- Russet mold in a **10-ft. square** within the cone is killed
- Creatures on fire are doused (fire damage ends)
- No damage to creatures

A full canister has **6 charges**. The one in the ropers' hoard has **3 charges remaining**.
