# Creative Writing — Mission Content Generation

## The Core Rule: Every Mission Hides a Truth

The job posted on the board is never the full story. The full story is what the DM knows. Players discover it through play.

```
WHAT PLAYERS SEE:        Recover a tithe ledger a courier failed to deliver.
WHAT'S ACTUALLY TRUE:    The "courier" is a financial officer who walked voluntarily.
                         She has the ledger as insurance against her own faction.
                         The ledger proves her superior is running a private skimming fund.
                         She wants it to reach someone who can actually use it.
```

This gap — between the posted job and the real situation — is where the mission lives.

---

## Two Formats, One Voice

Mission content appears in two formats:

1. **Board Posts** — Short Discord messages that hook players (50-150 words)
2. **Mission Modules** — Full DM documents with complete situation, NPCs, leads, consequences

Both should sound like they come from the same world.

---

## Mission Board Posts

### Structure

```
**[FACTION NAME] — MISSION TITLE**
*Tier: [tier] | Expires: [timeframe] | Reward: [X EC + extras]*
*Opposes: [faction or "None"]*

[2-3 sentences. The surface job only — never reveal the hidden truth here.]

*Contact: [named NPC], [location]*
```

### Title Rules

- Maximum 5 words. Concrete, specific, names the thing at stake.
- GOOD: "Track the Stolen Ledger", "Dust Market Strangler", "Pull Mira from Ashfield Hold"
- BAD: "The Reckoning", "Corruption Unraveled", "The Awakening Legacy" — abstract nouns that mean nothing

### Voice by Faction

Each faction posts missions in a distinct register:

| Faction | Voice | Real Example Hook |
|---------|-------|-------------------|
| **Iron Fang Consortium** | Transactional, professional, no emotional investment | "Payment on completion. Discretion premium included. No questions after." |
| **Wardens of Ash** | Military, terse, assumes compliance | "Standard sweep deviation. Backup requested. Details at briefing." |
| **Serpent Choir** | Formal, euphemistic, makes a favour sound like a contract | "Services rendered under standard compact terms. Discretion is assumed." |
| **Obsidian Lotus** | Never direct. Always deniable. | "A friend requires assistance with a personal matter. Usual place. Usual fee." |
| **Patchwork Saints** | Honest about what they can't afford | "We can't pay much. We need this done anyway. Someone has to." |
| **Glass Sigil** | Precise, specific, academically dry | "Collection required before degradation threshold. DC 14 recommended." |
| **Argent Blades** | Glory-forward, competitive | "First party to deliver claims the prize. The Blades will hear about it either way." |

### Board Post Example

```
✅ GOOD:
**Serpent Choir — Recover the Tithe Ledger**
*Tier: investigation | Expires: 6 days | Reward: 80 EC + Choir Blessing*
*Opposes: None*

A financial record in transit has gone missing. The document is sensitive.
Its contents concern internal Choir accounts — not something for public circulation.
Discretion is mandatory. Recovery must be clean.

*Contact: Brother Enn, Grand Forum Choir office*
```

Notice: the posted job reveals nothing about Sevas, her motives, or the corruption inside the Choir. That's the DM's truth.

## Full DM-Facing Mission Structure

Every generated module should contain these sections in this order:

### 1. Type + Reward Header
```
Type: Investigation / Recovery
Reward: [EC] + [bonus items if any]
Location: [specific named place]
Time Window: [how long before it expires]
Contact: [NPC name and where to find them]
```

### 2. Full Situation (DM-only truth)
This is the most important section. It tells the DM what is actually happening — not the surface job.

```
Full Situation: [NPC] is not who the board post implies. [2-4 sentences revealing the real
situation: who is actually involved, what they want, why they're doing what they're doing,
and what the players will discover if they dig.]

The [surface problem] exists because [real cause]. [Person] did [action] because [specific
human reason — fear, loyalty, desperation, ambition — not "evil" or "chaos".]
```

**Pattern from real missions:**
- Wex didn't steal the relic — he got frightened by what he'd carried and froze.
- Sevas didn't get kidnapped — she walked out voluntarily with the ledger as insurance.
- Orro isn't missing — he's being held by the knowledge of what disclosure would cost him.
- Elara isn't an illegal operation — she found a stable seam and is funding her research.

**The rule: NPCs have specific, understandable reasons for their behaviour. Not "they're bad" — because what specifically do they want? Because what specifically are they afraid of?**

### 3. Complications
```
Complications: [What can go wrong. Usually involves a second party with competing interests
who is not explicitly hostile but whose presence creates friction.]
```

Complications are not combat. They are situations that require a decision.
- A second crew is already looking for the same thing.
- The contact is under surveillance by someone who notices the party.
- The target isn't where expected — they moved.
- The item/person the party is looking for has already changed hands once.

### 4. Resolution Paths (non-combat primary)

Every mission needs at least two paths. The social/investigative path should always work and should be described first.

```
Resolution: [NPC] responds to [specific approach]:
- [Approach A]: What the NPC does if approached with [method]. DC [X] [skill] to [outcome].
- [Approach B]: What happens if [different method]. No DC — just read NPC's specific motivation.
- [Approach C]: If players pay / offer / leverage [specific thing], NPC [specific reaction].

WRONG path: Force / intimidation — [what breaks if they go this route].
```

**Real example (Wex):**
- He responds to honesty and sympathy.
- He will not hand over the disc to anyone there to punish him.
- A practical assurance (Sigil won't pursue action) makes him cooperative.
- 10 EC personal coin as a gesture: he hands it over and buys them a drink.
- Force/intimidation: he has nothing left to protect, so he talks anyway — but he's scared and may omit things.

### 5. DM Note (tone + mechanics + recurring NPC flag)
```
DM NOTE: [One sentence on emotional register — what is this mission about as a story?]
[One sentence on combat risk level.]
[Flag any recurring NPC with "is a recurring face if treated well."]
[Flag if this connects to another mission thread.]
```

**Real examples:**
- "Low-stakes, flavour-rich mission that rewards careful roleplay."
- "Pure social mission. No combat."
- "This is the most ethically complex mission on this board. Do not push the party toward any outcome."
- "Wex is a recurring face if treated well — a courier with Warrens connections."

### 6. Hook (seeds future content)
```
HOOK: [One specific thing the party learns or observes that will matter in a future mission.
They may not know it's significant yet. The DM should file it.]
```

**Real examples:**
- "The crystal's resonance matches a harmonic Dova has only seen in records of much larger Rift events — specifically, events that preceded major floor destabilisations."
- "The Lotus thread here is deliberately minor. The 'public recognition' reward is genuinely useful — a herald mentioning the party's name at the arena raises their profile in ways that compound over time."
- "If the ledger reaches Magister Liora, she will open a Tower Authority investigation. This takes months."

## Writing Investigation Leads

### Three-Clue Rule
For any critical revelation, provide THREE ways to discover it:
1. One obvious path (players do the expected thing)
2. One lateral path (creative thinking)
3. One fallback (NPC gives a hint when players are stuck)

Players will miss two of them. That's fine.

### Lead Structure
```
**Lead: [Name]**
**Where:** [Specific location]
**How to reach it:** [Skill check or approach — Social DC X, Investigation DC Y, bribe, lateral]
**What they find:** [Specific information, partial — creates a new question]
**What it leads to:** [Next location or NPC unlocked]
**What's misleading:** [The red herring embedded in this clue, if any]
```

### Good vs Bad Leads
```
❌ BAD:
Lead: The Clue
Source: They find it on the floor.
Reveals: The killer's name and address.

✅ GOOD (from real mission):
Lead: Wex's Ledger Drop
Source: He's been at the Adventurer's Inn for a week. DC 12 Grand Forum contacts to locate.
Reveals: He still has the disc. He's paralysed — knows it's worth more than his fee, scared.
Leads to: Negotiation scene. One-on-one, no Sigil colours showing works better.
Red herring: His evasiveness reads as guilt. He's not guilty — he's ashamed and afraid.
```

## Writing NPCs With Depth

Every NPC needs:
- **A specific reason for their behaviour** (not "they're corrupt" — why, what specifically)
- **Something they want from this conversation**
- **Something they will NOT give without the right approach**
- **A tell that signals their real state** (for Insight checks)

### NPC Format
```
**[NAME]** — [Role/Title]
**Situation:** [What they're actually dealing with right now — be specific]
**Surface:** [What they present to strangers]
**Real want:** [What they're actually trying to achieve]
**Hidden:** [What they won't say until trust is established or leverage applied]
**Responds to:** [What makes them cooperative — specific to this person]
**Insight DC [X]:** [What a successful read reveals]
**If threatened:** [Specific reaction — don't just say "resists"]
**Space tells:** [1-2 physical details in their space that speak before they do]
```

The "Space tells" field is critical for key NPCs. Players explore rooms before they meet the person. Let the room answer questions.

```
✅ GOOD:
Neses (medusa priestess, from community module)
Space tells: Two child-sized dolls on the floor. One normal pillow, one child-sized, on the bed.
A letter from the city council (found on desk) threatening death by hanging for heresy.

Reading these before meeting Neses means players understand her before she speaks.
Her anger isn't performance — they've seen the evidence.
```

### Example (Wex, from real mission)
```
WEX — Courier
Situation: Delivered a package he shouldn't have opened. Now recognises it's valuable.
Has been paralysed at the Adventurer's Inn for a week, spending the delivery fee.
Surface: Evasive, deflecting, buys rounds to fill silences.
Real want: Reassurance that he's not in trouble. Practical, specific reassurance.
Hidden: He still has the disc. He wants someone to take the decision off him.
Responds to: Honesty. Sympathy. Specific assurances (not vague ones). 10 EC gesture.
Insight DC 13: He's not calculating — he's ashamed and scared. He wants out.
If threatened: Cooperates immediately. Has nothing left to protect. May omit details.
```

## The Micro-Detail that Unlocks an NPC

Every minor NPC — not just major contacts — benefits from one specific life detail that makes them real.

```
❌ BAD:
Alron is a bandit leader who wants to rob the party.

✅ GOOD (from community module):
Alron is 55 and wants to retire. He has saved up almost enough gold for a tavern in a land 
where he's not a wanted criminal. One or two more robberies and he'll be set.
"Listen up fellers, we can do this the easy way or the hard way. Personally, I'd hate to 
hurt you oldies."
He means it. This is inconvenient for him. He would prefer the party just handed over 
their packs and walked on.
```

One specific life-detail makes the difference:
- **Wex:** delivery fee spent, paralysed by indecision for a week
- **Alron:** almost enough gold for a tavern, wants out
- **Sevas:** found records accidentally, doesn't know who to trust
- **Brother Enn:** nervous during handoff — observable, Insight DC 13

The detail tells players: this NPC is a person in a situation. Intimidation may work differently than expected. Sympathy may open a door. One sentence of backstory does more than a paragraph of personality description.

## Villain Motivation Template

A villain is an NPC with a grievance, a goal, and a method. The grievance must be traceable and human.

```
Structure:
GRIEVANCE: [Specific harm done to them, by whom, that the players can verify.]
GOAL:      [What they want — specific, not "more power"]
METHOD:    [What they're doing to get it, and what it costs others]
LOGIC:     [Why their method makes sense to them — even if it doesn't to the party]

✅ GOOD (from community module):
GRIEVANCE: City council declared her goddess heretical, sent soldiers, killed her daughter.
GOAL:      Resurrect Ada.
METHOD:    Necromancy ritual draining the city's farmland of life force.
LOGIC:     The city that killed her daughter can provide what it takes to bring her back.
           She does not see this as disproportionate.

✅ GOOD (Tower canon parallel):
GRIEVANCE: Zariel was a divine commander who fell. Montreval is a mortal commander 
           who was destroyed and placed on the machine that killed everything he built.
GOAL:      She wants to see if someone can do what she could not — reach him in time.
METHOD:    Offers a boon with a genuine hook — she wants to see what the party will 
           sacrifice and will collect that answer later.
LOGIC:     She recognises the shape of Montreval's story because it is her story.
           Her interest is not sympathy. It is recognition.
```

**The key:** Players should be able to read the villain's grievance from physical evidence in the world — a letter, a room, a document — before the villain explains it. Explanation after evidence lands. Explanation alone is backstory.

## Cross-Mission Threading

Great posting periods have a quiet through-line — something that appears in multiple missions without being named explicitly. Players who do multiple jobs may notice the pattern. If they surface it, it becomes the next major event.

**Real example (entire posting period):**
An unusual Rift signature appears in four separate missions:
- Glass Sigil: crystalline residue with a strange harmonic
- Wardens: anomalous residue in sewer grid seven
- Iron Fang: vendor selling material from an unlisted source
- Adventurers Guild: wall survey returns a reading that doesn't match post-surge pattern

No single mission makes the connection explicit. Dova (Glass Sigil) is closest to figuring it out. If the party reports their findings to her, she can synthesise it. If they don't, she figures it out later — during a crisis.

**How to thread:**
1. Pick one element that appears in 2-4 missions (a name, a residue signature, a faction move, a missing person)
2. Each mission surfaces it once, in its own context, without labelling it as connected
3. The DM NOTE in each mission flags: "This connects to [other mission]. Do not make the link explicit yet."
4. File the synthesis for the next posting period or a major event

## Resolution & Consequences

Every mission must have:
1. **Success path** — What concretely changes if they succeed
2. **Failure path** — What the antagonist achieves if they fail
3. **The moral choice** — At least one mission per posting period should have no clean outcome

### Real Resolution Example (Tithe Ledger)
```
The Choice:
- Return the ledger to the Choir as instructed: 80 EC, Choir blessing, Sevas is "handled"
  by the Choir, Brother Enn's operation continues.
- Deliver it to the Scrolls or Liora as Sevas requests: no Choir payment, potential reward
  from Scrolls/Liora, Choir standing drops to Unfriendly, Brother Enn's operation unravels
  over months.
- Keep the ledger: very risky, very valuable, makes three parties unhappy simultaneously.

DM NOTE: Do not push the party toward any outcome. Let them make the call.
```

Note the specificity: named consequences, named NPCs affected, named standing changes, named future consequences.

## Quick Reference

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Board title** | ≤5 words, concrete noun, names what's at stake |
| **Board post** | Surface job only — never reveal the hidden truth |
| **Full situation** | DM-only truth — NPC real motivation, specific not vague |
| **Complications** | Second party with competing interests, not combat |
| **Resolution** | Non-combat path described first, always |
| **DM Note** | Tone + combat level + recurring NPC flag |
| **Hook** | One specific thing that will matter later |
| **NPCs** | Specific reason + specific want + specific response |
| **Micro-detail** | One life-specific sentence per NPC (Alron: almost enough for a tavern) |
| **Space tells** | 2-3 physical objects in NPC space that speak before they do |
| **Villain** | Grievance + goal + method + why it makes sense to them |
| **Threading** | One element per posting that appears in 2-4 missions |

**The mission is not the job. The mission is what the players discover when they do the job.**
